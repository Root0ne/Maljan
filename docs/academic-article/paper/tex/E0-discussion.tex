\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Most of these negative results are bounds rather than equivalences, which was not evident when they
were first written down. Five of the studies run on five fixtures, where the sign-flip floor of
Equation~\eqref{eq:floor} puts $\alpha$=0.05 out of reach whatever the effect size, which is why
every null here is reported beside the effect its design could have detected. It also changes what
the cheap fix is. On seeing an underpowered paired design the
instinct is to add repeats, because repeats are cheap and samples are expensive; on clustered data
that instinct is exactly wrong. A sixth fixture would have halved the attainable p, and a sixth
repeat on five fixtures moves it not at all.

The literature we counter-searched against locates silent failures at the boundary between modules,
and four of our seven crossed such a boundary while three crossed none. What the seven share is not
a boundary but an attribution: an ablation varied a deterministic reconciliation step and we
attributed the difference to a language model, a rank correlation ordered a configuration flag and
we attributed it to parameter count, and a renamed output ceiling let us attribute boundedness to a
component that had never once been bounded. In every case the number was real and the arrow pointing
to its cause was drawn by us. Seven cases from one project is not a class, so we offer this as a
proposal rather than a taxonomy, but it suggests a different question from the one
boundary-oriented thinking asks. Not whether a component failed silently; instead, for each number
about to be reported, whether its cause was measured or assigned. The clustered-inference correction
of Section~\ref{sec:method} is that question turned on ourselves and answered badly: every interval
in an earlier draft attributed its width to sampling variation among independent observations, the
observations were not independent, and nothing in the pipeline, the tests or the review caught it,
because the defect was never in a function.

What we are not in a position to say needs the same precision. Negotiation is not shown to be
worthless, model capacity is not shown to be off the ceiling, and verbal confidence is not shown to
be uninformative; each holds only at this design's resolution, on this corpus. Nor are our seven
mechanisms shown to generalise. What we can say is what each of them cost.
