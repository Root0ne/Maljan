\subsection{Five objections}\label{objections}

We wrote down the five strongest objections we could raise against this paper and
then answered them, or failed to and said so. Three are answered by measurements
already reported. Two are not answerable with what we have, and each is stated
below with the experiment that would settle it.

\subsubsection{The central experiment's corpus is generated from its own answer
key}\label{obj-fixtures}

\textbf{The objection.} The equal-budget consensus comparison, the frontier
comparison and the confidence calibration all run on \fact{fixture-clusters}
synthesised fixtures whose evidence is assembled by walking each fixture's
ground-truth technique list and emitting one hand-written sentence per technique.
Evidence and answer key are in bijection. A deterministic regular expression over
the dictionary that produced that evidence scores F1 \fact{fixture-extractor-f1}
on \fact{fixture-extractor-n} of \fact{fixture-extractor-n}. Whatever those arms
measure, it is not what a pipeline does to a binary.

\textbf{We concede it, and it changes what those arms are for.} They are a pilot.
They isolate one variable at a time under conditions where nothing else can move,
which is worth something for a mechanism question --- does the negotiation
aggregate or reconcile, does the flag consume the answer budget --- and worth
nothing for an accuracy claim. We report no accuracy claim from them: the
pipeline's accuracy is stated only against the no-LLM baseline on real samples
(Table~\ref{tab:results-2}), where the two are scored on one population against
one ground truth.

The corpus also bounds inference in a way no amount of data collection inside it
would relieve. With \fact{fixture-clusters} clusters, the exact two-sided cluster
permutation test cannot return a value below \fact{fixture-signflip-floor}, so no
comparison measured there can reach $\alpha$ = 0.05 whatever its effect size. Adding
decoding repeats does not help --- repeats add rows inside clusters, and the test
counts clusters.

\textbf{What would settle it.} Running the same three arms on the
\fact{baseline-n}-sample cohort, which has real binaries, sandbox reports,
family-level ground truth and \fact{baseline-families} clusters. The evidence
builder for that exists and is released with this paper; the arms are not run
because the model server does not fit on this host alongside anything else, which
is Section~\ref{sec:threats}'s own finding rather than an excuse.

\subsubsection{The system does not beat its baseline, so the component analysis
is uninteresting}\label{obj-baseline}

\textbf{The objection.} On the samples where both were run, the full pipeline
scores \fact{h2h-dynamic-f1} against the sandbox's own signature mapping at
\fact{h2h-cape-f1} --- a paired difference of \fact{h2h-delta},
\fact{h2h-ci}. If the whole system is indistinguishable from the deterministic
tool it wraps, then of course its components move nothing, and a paper of
component-level nulls inside a system that does not work is a paper about
nothing.

\textbf{We answer it, but only partly.} The inference is not valid in the
direction the objection runs: an end-to-end null does not entail component-level
nulls, and two of the components measured here are not inert but
\emph{suppressed} --- the corroboration cascade is computed and then discarded at
the reconciliation step, which is a mechanism finding that would hold at any
end-to-end score. The reverse inference is the one we are careful about, and we
do not make it.

Where the objection lands is on what the paper is for. It is not a systems paper
and says so: the architecture did not survive its own evaluation, and the
contribution is the account of measuring it. A reader who wants a system that
beats its baseline will not find one here.

\subsubsection{One of the retrieval numbers is self-consistency, not
accuracy}\label{obj-circularity}

\textbf{The objection.} The ATT\&CK case-prior index is reported as working in
isolation at F1 0.620 against a 0.424 frequency prior. That evaluation is
leave-one-out over a corpus of 1,733 cases whose labels are \emph{the pipeline's
own prior attributions}. Scoring a retriever against the labels its own upstream
produced measures agreement with itself.

\textbf{We concede it and have relabelled it.} The number is self-consistency and
is now presented as such. It is retained rather than deleted because the
comparison it supports is still meaningful --- a frequency prior scored the same
way reaches 0.424, so the index carries information beyond base rates even under
its own labelling --- and because the production number beside it is scored
against MITRE ground truth on held-out samples, where the index loses to the
prior (0.111 against 0.123). That second number is the one the paper's conclusion
rests on.

We also ran the leakage check the objection implies rather than asserting the
corpus is disjoint: no sha256 in the case corpus appears in either evaluation
cohort. The construct problem is the labelling, not overlap.

\subsubsection{The judge findings rest on four calls}\label{obj-small-n}

\textbf{The objection.} The paper's most quoted architectural result --- that the
judge supplies \fact{judge-capped-own-ids} of \fact{judge-capped-bundle}
techniques when it works and that \fact{fallback-only-capped} uncorroborated
techniques reach the analyst when it fails --- is measured on four judge calls.
Four. The mechanism check that generalises it covers
\fact{judge-mechanism-samples} samples. A reviewer is entitled to ask why, with
\fact{baseline-n} samples and archived reports for all of them, the study that
carries the headline used four.

\textbf{We concede the sample size and defend the design, and they are different
things.} The intercept is expensive: it patches the reconciliation seam and the
text-fallback builder and records every technique crossing each, which requires a
live judge call per observation and produced the run this paper reports. Four is
what the quota and the host allowed.

What the four support is narrower than the sentence they are usually quoted in.
On the working path the finding is deductive rather than statistical --- the
reconciliation step restores the cascade's set by construction, and the
\fact{judge-mechanism-arms}-arm mechanism check confirms the bundle equals the
cascade set on every arm, with the judge contributing one of its own on
\fact{judge-mechanism-added} of \fact{judge-mechanism-samples} samples --- an
upper bound of \fact{judge-mechanism-upper-bound} on the per-sample rate. On the failing path it is an existence claim: uncorroborated identifiers
\emph{do} reach the analyst, demonstrated on four calls with an uncapped control
whose raw text provably contains none. Neither claim is a rate, and the paper
should not be read as offering one.

\textbf{What would settle it.} The same intercept over the
\fact{baseline-n}-sample cohort. It needs no new instrumentation, only the host.

\subsubsection{Seven defects in your own code is a post-mortem, not a
result}\label{obj-postmortem}

\textbf{The objection.} A paper reporting that its authors made seven mistakes is
a claim about those authors. A more careful team would have avoided them; the
mechanisms are ordinary; and nothing here shows they recur elsewhere.

\textbf{This is the objection we can least answer, and we do not claim the
category.} Every mechanism was counter-searched in an adjacent field's vocabulary
before being claimed, and the search demoted more than it confirmed: silent
failure at tool boundaries is documented and taxonomised, the metamorphic
relation behind our detector is ordinary, and that an inference-time constraint
can invert model rankings is established elsewhere with a larger effect than
ours. What we assert is narrower than a class: a setting, where the artefact of
such a failure is not a degraded user session but a measurement that is wrong and
looks right, and a price paid rather than hypothesised.

Two things we cannot show. We cannot show these recur in other teams' pipelines,
because we have one pipeline. And we cannot show our detector generalises: it
found four defects, but it found them retrospectively, on batches we already had
reason to distrust. A prospective test --- report output cardinality beside every
batch for a year and count how many defects it catches before anything else does
--- is the experiment, and we have not run it.

\subsubsection{What the five have in common}\label{obj-common}

Three of the five are answered by measurements, and the two that are not
(\ref{obj-fixtures} and \ref{obj-small-n}) are blocked by the same thing: a host
on which the model server does not fit alongside anything else. That is not a
coincidence and it is not a resource complaint. Section~\ref{sec:threats} reports
the environment as an uncontrolled variable that cost four arms of a completed
ablation; here it is the reason two of this paper's weakest results are weak. An
evaluation's ceiling is set by its machine at least as often as by its method,
and we would rather state which of our limits are which.
