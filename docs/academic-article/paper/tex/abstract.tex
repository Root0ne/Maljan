Multi-agent LLM pipelines map malware evidence to MITRE ATT\&CK techniques, and their components are
rarely measured against the deterministic tooling they are built on. This study evaluates one such
pipeline and reports both its measurements and the failures of the instrument that produced them. A no-LLM baseline, the sandbox's own signature mapping, reaches F1
\fact{baseline-f1} \fact{baseline-f1-cluster-ci} on \fact{baseline-n} samples. On the
\fact{h2h-n} of those where both arms ran, scored per sample against the same ground truth by the
same code, the full pipeline reaches \fact{h2h-dynamic-f1} against that baseline's
\fact{h2h-cape-f1}, paired \fact{h2h-delta} \fact{h2h-ci}, at a resolution of \fact{h2h-mde}. On \fact{cape-consensus-families} real samples, one per family, negotiated
multi-agent consensus beats a single judge by \fact{cape-negotiated-delta} F1
\fact{cape-negotiated-ci}, and so does a stochastic-noise control, by \fact{cape-noise-delta}; the
two differ by \fact{cape-mechanism-delta} at a resolution of \fact{mde-cape-mechanism}, so what
helps is the calls the arms share, not the negotiation. Verbal confidence, which
every deterministic gate consumes, ranks correct claims above incorrect ones at AUC
\fact{confidence-auc}, on an interval containing chance. Seven defects in our own instrument
produced plausible results rather than errors and \fact{test-count} passing tests caught none: four
crossed a boundary with another server, and three share a different shape, a measurement's cause
assigned rather than measured. The largest is this paper's own statistics, every interval having
resampled rows when the observations were clustered, whose correction widened the anchor interval
\fact{baseline-f1-widening}-fold and turned two equivalence claims into bounds.
