Multi-agent LLM pipelines map malware evidence to MITRE ATT\&CK techniques, and their components are
rarely measured against the deterministic tooling they sit on. We evaluate one, and report why
several of our own measurements of it were wrong.

Scored per sample on the same binaries and ground truth, the full pipeline reaches F1
\fact{h2h-dynamic-f1} against \fact{h2h-cape-f1} for the sandbox it is built on, paired
\fact{h2h-delta} \fact{h2h-ci}, at a resolution of \fact{h2h-mde}. On
\fact{cape-consensus-families} real samples, one per family, negotiated multi-agent consensus beats
a single judge by \fact{cape-negotiated-delta} F1 \fact{cape-negotiated-ci}, and so does a noise
control, by \fact{cape-noise-delta}. The two differ by \fact{cape-mechanism-delta} at a resolution
of \fact{mde-cape-mechanism}. What helps is the calls the arms share, not the negotiation. Verbal
confidence, which every deterministic gate consumes, ranks correct claims above incorrect ones at
AUC \fact{confidence-auc}, on an interval containing chance.

Seven defects in our own instrument produced plausible results rather than errors, and
\fact{test-count} passing tests caught none. Four crossed a boundary with another server; the other
three share a shape, a measurement's cause assigned rather than measured. The
largest is this paper's own statistics. Every interval we published resampled rows when the
observations were clustered, and correcting it widened our anchor interval
\fact{baseline-f1-widening}-fold and turned two equivalence claims into bounds.

We contribute the measured negatives with the minimum detectable effect beside each, seven failure
mechanisms with what found them, and a no-LLM baseline (F1 \fact{baseline-f1}, n=\fact{baseline-n})
without which none of these numbers means anything.
