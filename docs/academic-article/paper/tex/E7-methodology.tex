\subsection{Samples, ground truth and the sandbox}\label{samples-ground-truth-and-the-sandbox}

The dynamic cohort is n=\fact{cohort-n} Windows PE samples from MalwareBazaar, stratified by year at
seed \fact{cohort-seed} with its digest recorded, of which \fact{baseline-n} produced a usable
sandbox report. Ground truth resolves a family signature to an in-repo MITRE \texttt{uses} fixture
through an alias map with a CamelCase-split fallback. It is family-level and uncurated at technique
level, so a single binary of a family need not exhibit all the techniques catalogued for that family
and absolute recall carries a structural ceiling. Absolute F1 values here are therefore not
comparable to work using per-sample expert labels; the bias is approximately constant across arms,
which makes within-study contrasts the defensible reading. The
withdrawn temporal-drift study's manifest survives as \fact{drift-n} dated samples over
\fact{drift-cohorts} cohorts and \fact{drift-families} families, metadata only.

Three properties of the sandbox bound what can be reproduced. It is CAPE~2.5, the Config and Payload Extraction sandbox, with one analysis machine and no Linux
guest. An Executable and Linkable Format binary submitted there therefore joins the
\fact{cape-backlog} tasks that are never scheduled. Reports are retained for days rather than indefinitely, with
\fact{cape-report-retention-sampled} of \fact{cape-report-retention-sampled} sampled older tasks
reporting no report directory, so the local archive is the only copy and re-submitting the same
samples is not a reproduction recipe on its own. And the instance rate-limits with a throttle response that is byte-identical to its refusal of an
unauthorised request: \texttt{\{"error":\ true,\ "message":\ ""\}} in both cases. A harness that
reads that string as a verdict on its own permissions will therefore mistake a throttle for a
missing one, as ours did briefly. A new submission is scheduled in \textasciitilde1 second
and completes in \textasciitilde5.5 minutes, putting an n=\fact{cohort-n} cohort at roughly
\fact{cohort-sandbox-hours} hours of sandbox time.

Two serving details matter to anyone reproducing an arm. The local server's output cap must be sent twice. \texttt{ChatOpenAI(max\_tokens=N)} reaches the
wire as \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens}, which \texttt{ik\_llama.cpp} does not read, so the cap
must also travel in \texttt{extra\_body}. Measured on this host, \fact{cap-requested} requested
yields \fact{cap-ignored-tokens} generated with the renamed field alone, and
\fact{cap-honoured-tokens} with both. And that server truncates silently, returning \texttt{finish\_reason:\ "stop"} at the cap and
never \texttt{"length"}, so telemetry detecting truncation by finish reason reports zero however
often the cap binds. The frontier arm ran on a free tier allowing \fact{openrouter-daily-limit}
requests per day at \fact{openrouter-burst-limit} per minute, and the first attempt exhausted it
mid-flight with \fact{quota-calls-landed} calls landing and \fact{quota-calls-refused} returning

reachable on that tier at all.

\subsection{Experimental design}\label{experimental-design}

Four terms are used in one sense throughout. An \emph{arm} is one condition of one experiment, a
configuration under which every input is scored, so that two arms differ in the one thing being
tested. A \emph{sample} is a real malware binary from the dated cohort, scored against its family's
MITRE \texttt{uses} set. A \emph{fixture} is one of the five synthesised inputs used by the equal-budget arms. Its evidence
is generated from its own technique list, so it has a ceiling a real sample does not. Samples and
fixtures are different populations with different ground truth. They are never pooled, and they are
never drawn on one axis. The
\emph{reconciliation step} is the deterministic pass that compares the judge's bundle against the
corroboration cascade's set and restores what the judge omitted.

Arms are compared at an equal total token budget: a multi-agent arm making N calls receives B/N per
call against a single arm's one call of B, so a win cannot come from spending more. The design
follows Bertalanič and Fortuna \cite{bertalanic2026consensus}, including a stochastic-noise control
in which one analyst receives evidence irrelevant to the sample. That control establishes that the
harness can detect a difference at all: in the fixture consensus study the noise arm separated
(\fact{consensus-noise-f1} against \fact{consensus-negotiated-f1} and \fact{consensus-single-f1})
while the treatment did not, which distinguishes no effect from no sensitivity. For a reasoning model the budget must cover the reasoning as well. Our frontier endpoint spent
\fact{arm-default-reasoning} of its output on reasoning across \fact{arm-default-n} real prompts,
and \fact{reasoning-share-one-token} on a one-token answer. Capping the answer alone would have
granted it roughly twice the generation for the same nominal budget. Arms must also sit on the same side of the reasoning switch. Measured on two models at
\fact{arm-default-n} calls each, enabling the flag moved F1 by \fact{reasoning-flag-third-delta-2dp}
on one and \fact{reasoning-flag-vendor35b-delta-2dp} on the other. The cause is the same in both:
the model spends its entire output budget thinking and returns no answer. On the second,
\fact{arm-qwen35ba3b-hit-cap} of \fact{arm-qwen35ba3b-n} calls hit the cap. An unmatched pair therefore does not
produce a noisy comparison; it produces a comparison of the flag.

Three further rules were adopted after each one caught something, and all three are cheap. The first is that a mechanism's firing rate is measured before its effect. The confidence cap fires
on \fact{cap-rate} of techniques, so an ablation of it would mostly describe the
\fact{cap-rate-complement} where it never ran. The sink-reachability hint fires on \fact{hint-rate}
of samples, so an ablation of that one does say something about the hint. The cap's preconditions explain its rate, since it applies to three
technique families only when the sole contributing layer is static and
\fact{cap-gated-yara-only-share} of those claims are YARA-only. An ablation must therefore be
restricted to the samples where the mechanism fires, because averaging a feature over inputs it
never touched dilutes a real effect toward zero. The second is that the number of distinct outputs
the N inputs produced is counted and reported as a result column rather than as a test, because the
signal exists
only in the batch while each individual response is well-formed; we report
\fact{cardinality-distinct-graphs} distinct call-graph sizes across \fact{cardinality-samples}
samples. The third is that per-sample outputs are retained for every study, since aggregates cannot
be re-interrogated:
when a defect was found that might have affected a completed \fact{drift-n}-sample study, the
question could not be asked because only the summary survived. Long-lived servers accumulate state
that crosses sample boundaries, so the reverse-engineering server is restarted between samples,
which recovered \fact{switch-recovered} of \fact{switch-attempted} samples previously recorded as
unmeasurable. Two audits were added for the same reason: diffing the claim register against
the evidence log, and counter-searching each novelty claim in an adjacent field's vocabulary, which
demoted five including the one central to this paper. A counter-search is recorded whether or not it
demotes anything, because a searched absence is only credible if the looking is documented.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}\label{statistical-analysis}

Every arm is scored on the same samples in the same order. Differences are computed per sample and
then aggregated, and 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) are taken over the paired deltas. The
observations are nested: many claims come from one sample, and many samples from one family. The
bootstrap therefore resamples whole clusters rather than rows, and the p-value comes from flipping
the sign of whole cluster means. Each interval is reported with the intraclass correlation
coefficient (ICC) of its measure and the effective sample size that implies. Two properties of that test are reported with it. With $k$ clusters the
two-sided version cannot return a p below
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
p_{\min} = 2 \big/ 2^{k}
\end{equation}
At $k$=\setting{5} that is \fact{fixture-signflip-floor}, which puts $\alpha$=0.05 out of reach
whatever the effect size. Above \setting{20} clusters the $2^k$ assignments are sampled rather than
enumerated, at \fact{stats-iters} draws, with the observed assignment counted in its own reference
set. The smallest p that route can return is therefore one over the draws rather than zero. Each reported p names which
route produced it. The two properties pull in opposite directions: raising the cluster count lifts
the floor and is what makes enumeration stop being possible, which is why the consensus arms were
re-run one sample per family. Multiplicity is controlled with Benjamini-Hochberg over two
declared families, and every null is reported with the minimum detectable effect of the design that
produced it at 80\% power, because a null without its resolution reads either as equivalence or as a
failed experiment depending on the reader.

Both choices were corrections rather than plans: every interval in an earlier draft resampled rows
rather than clusters, and an early study ranked prompt structures on a single-run parsed-claim count
whose ranking inverted when the decoding budget changed. No result in this paper rests on a single
run.
