\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsection{Mapping evidence to ATT\&CK}\label{mapping-evidence-to-attck}

Language models are already applied across the malware task surface: tracking malicious packages in
a registry \cite{yu2024maltracker}, detecting Android malware from application features
\cite{zhao2025apppoet} and from generated training samples \cite{rollinson2026samples}, and
summarising what a sample does in prose \cite{saha2025malaware}. None of these produces ATT\&CK
technique identifiers from a running analysis of a Windows binary, which is the task this pipeline
attempts and the reason the work below is the nearer comparison.

Automated ATT\&CK technique extraction has moved from supervised classifiers such as TRAM \cite{mitre2023tram}, rcATT \cite{legoy2020rcatt} and TTPDrill \cite{husari2017ttpdrill}, through
neural extractors such as EXTRACTOR \cite{satvat2021extractor}, AttacKG \cite{li2022attackg} and
LADDER \cite{alam2023ladder}, to
retrieval-augmented generation. TechniqueRAG \cite{lekssays2025techniquerag} pairs off-the-shelf retrievers with an
instruction-tuned re-ranker and fine-tunes only the generator, motivated by the field's data
scarcity. ATT\&CK defines over 550 (sub-)techniques while roughly 10,000 annotated examples exist
publicly. A \emph{separate} group's hierarchical successor, H-TechniqueRAG \preprintcite{morbiato2026hierarchical}, injects the tactic$\rightarrow$technique taxonomy as an inductive bias, filtering at tactic level first to cut the candidate space by 77.5\%.
It also reports an F1 gain and a latency reduction over TechniqueRAG. We do not restate those two
figures. They are unreviewed and self-reported against a baseline that the SoK below finds
``largely incomparable'' to its neighbours, so quoting them here would contradict the paragraph
after this one. TTPrint \preprintcite{cheng2026ttprint} takes an orthogonal route, decomposing reports into atomic
behaviours and retaining only candidates supported by both localised evidence spans and the
MITRE definition.

Büchel et al.'s SoK \cite{buchel2025sok} is the field's own assessment. Re-evaluating 40+ systems in a unified
setting, they report a performance ceiling existing approaches have not crossed, that existing
solutions are ``largely incomparable'' because they use custom ontologies and inaccessible
datasets, and, counterintuitively, that traditional NLP approaches outperform modern
embedder-based and generative approaches in realistic settings.

Position. Our describe-then-map design removes technique-ID recall from the model entirely.
The analyst describes behaviour and a deterministic index assigns the identifier. This is a
domain instantiation of Infer-Retrieve-Rank \preprintcite{doosterlinck2024incontext}, which established the general program, multi-step LM/retriever interaction that decouples inference from assignment over a
many-thousand-class label space, reaching state of the art on three benchmarks with tens of
labelled examples. Ours is stricter than \preprintcite{doosterlinck2024incontext} and than \cite{lekssays2025techniquerag}. In both of those the language model
still ranks or selects among retrieved candidates, whereas here it never emits an identifier and
any correction is gated to the provably-safe invalid$\rightarrow$valid case. We do not claim the idea.

What we contribute is a decomposition of the SoK's insight. \cite{buchel2025sok} reports that embedders lose; we
measure \emph{where}. Ranking quality and gate quality behave as separable axes here. Dense embeddings
rank better while their scores barely separate a correct pick from a wrong one, and a lexical
index ranks worse while gating cleanly. Composing the per-axis winners beats either pure backend
on both, measured on TRAM2 \cite{mitre2023tram} (N=4,913) and replicated on the independently annotated AnnoCTR
corpus \cite{lange2024annoctr}.

The separation itself is not ours, and an earlier draft of this section claimed it was.
Abdallah et al.~\cite{abdallah2026retrievers} evaluate retrieval confidence, AUROC for predicting query success, as a
dimension explicitly distinct from retrieval effectiveness, find calibration ``consistently weak
across model families'', and conclude raw scores are ``unreliable for downstream routing without
additional calibration''. That is the general statement, over more retrievers than we test. What
remains ours is narrower and specific: the inversion, that in this task the \emph{lexical} backend
is the better gate while the \emph{semantic} one is the better ranker, so the two axes have different
winners and can be composed. We found that inversion neither asserted nor excluded elsewhere,
which is a weaker absence than the one we first claimed, and we say so.

We also report a result about correction policy. Auto-correcting \emph{valid but weakly-aligned}
identifiers damages 38\% of already-correct labels to recover 21\% of wrong ones. The asymmetry is
not a discovery: grammatical error correction has studied over-correction for years and
evaluates with F0.5 precisely because a false correction costs more than a missed one \cite{ng2014conll,bryant2019bea}, and
recent work targets the failure directly. Our contribution is the \emph{mechanism} in this setting:
correct-but-weak and wrong-but-valid identifiers are not separable by an alignment score, so
the valid$\rightarrow$valid swap cannot be safely tuned at any error rate, which is why the shipped policy is
restricted to the invalid$\rightarrow$valid case, where zero regression holds by construction rather than by
tuning.

Finally, the failure that motivates removing identifier recall in the first place. Asked for an
ATT\&CK identifier it does not know, the model enters a degenerate loop, proposing and re-proposing
wrong identifiers until the budget is gone. The loop is not novel and neither is the inadequacy
of the obvious remedy: neural text degeneration is a studied phenomenon with competing
established accounts. Welleck et al.~locate the cause in the \emph{likelihood objective itself}, which
``assigns too much probability to sequences containing repeats'', while a later data-side analysis
finds a strong correlation with repetition already present in the training data and argues that
the earlier self-reinforcement explanations reduce to it \cite{welleck2019unlikelihood,li2023repetition}. The limits of a uniform repetition
penalty are known either way; it damages tokens that legitimately recur. We reproduce
that here and can add one engine-level detail, that the server honours \texttt{repeat\_penalty} while
silently ignoring three sibling parameters, which converts a tight loop into a slower enumeration
without fixing it.

What we can contribute is the consequence. The degeneration literature measures repetition
rate and text quality; here the budget is exhausted inside an operational deadline, the verdict
stage times out, and the analyst receives an empty STIX bundle, degeneration as a failure to
\emph{deliver}, not a failure of prose. We measure that channel directly in Section~\ref{what-the-ablations-cost-and-what-that-revealed}, where empty bundles fall from 6/17 to 1/17. Constrained decoding would also solve the original loop, and we say so; the point is
that the cheaper mitigation a practitioner reaches for first does not.

\subsection{LLM agents over binaries}\label{llm-agents-over-binaries}

Agentic systems now drive reverse-engineering backends through tool interfaces, and the model
tier splits sharply by role. Fine-tuned binary specialists are overwhelmingly small and
open-weight, among them ReCopilot \preprintcite{chen2025recopilot} on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, LLM4Decompile \cite{tan2024llm4decompile} at 1.3--33B and Nova \cite{jiang2025nova}, while the
\emph{tool-driving} agents use frontier models. TTPDetect \preprintcite{ttpdetect2026} is the closest system to ours: it
identifies TTPs in stripped malware binaries using dense plus neural retrieval to narrow
analysis entry points, then a function-level agent with incremental context retrieval, reporting
93.25\% precision and 93.81\% recall at function level and 87.37\% precision on real-world malware.

Position. We do not claim binary evidence as an input modality; \preprintcite{ttpdetect2026} holds that ground. Two
differences remain and only one is currently defensible. First, \preprintcite{ttpdetect2026} appears to demonstrate on a
cloud model while our pipeline runs on a \textasciitilde3B-active local MoE, a deployment claim, addressed in
Section~\ref{what-runs-it} rather than here. Second, \preprintcite{ttpdetect2026} reasons at function level over decompiled code while we fuse
six deterministic detectors, three domain analysts and a corroboration cascade; that is a
different system, but ``different'' is not a contribution until the difference is measured.

The one gap we do occupy is tool-catalogue scale. Degradation of tool selection as the catalogue
grows is documented generally. LongFuncEval \preprintcite{kate2025longfunceval} reports a 7--85\% performance drop as the tool count
increases, and reducing an edge model's catalogue from 46 to 19 tools cuts execution time by up to 70\% \cite{paramanayakam2025lessismore}, but has not been measured for a reverse-engineering catalogue, where schemas are large
and tools semantically overlap. Our finding that exposing \textasciitilde201 Ghidra MCP tools ($\approx$22k tokens of
schema) is infeasible at this model scale, motivating a curated 20-tool allowlist with
high-value tools invoked deterministically from code, is the domain instance. It is a single
feasibility point rather than a degradation curve, and we say so.

\subsection{Multi-agent consensus}\label{multi-agent-consensus}

Multi-agent debate has become a standard pattern, including in security. PhishDebate
\cite{li2025phishdebate} assigns four specialised agents to distinct views of a webpage under a
moderator and judge, reporting 98.2\% recall and improvements over single-agent and
chain-of-thought baselines.

Two 2026 results change how such claims must be read. Tran and Kiela \preprintcite{tran2026single} hold the reasoning
token budget constant and find single agents consistently match or beat multi-agent systems on
multi-hop reasoning, supported by an information-theoretic argument from the Data Processing
Inequality. Under a fixed budget with good context utilisation, decomposition introduces
communication bottlenecks that lose information. Bertalanič and Fortuna \cite{bertalanic2026consensus} reach the same
conclusion on 7--8B open-weight models, where debate consumed 2.1--3.4$\times$ more tokens for equal or
lower accuracy, and name three failure modes, namely sycophantic conformity (modal adoption up to
85.5\%), contextual fragility, and consensus collapse discarding correct answers already present
in the generation pool.

Position. We do not claim that multi-agent analysis beats a single agent, and we report the
equal-budget comparison rather than assuming it. Both \cite{bertalanic2026consensus} and \preprintcite{tran2026single} scope their negative results
to \emph{homogeneous} agents decomposing a \emph{single} context, and both name the exception, degraded
or noisy context, which is the regime a 600-import binary plus a sandbox report plus network
capture occupies. Whether heterogeneous evidence-channel decomposition survives the control that
homogeneous debate fails is the question our ablation asks.

Our contribution here is a failure mode adjacent to \cite{bertalanic2026consensus}'s. Sycophantic conformity arises from
majority pressure among agents holding the same information. We observed a distinct mechanism:
an analyst whose evidence channel was empty paraphrased its peers, and because the echo
returned tagged with that analyst's own domain, the corroboration counter read it as independent
confirmation, promoting a single-source finding to \emph{corroborated} and weighting it above the
source it was copied from. In a security pipeline the harm is not a wrong answer but a
fabricated independent confirmation.

Finally, weighting evidence by source reliability and discounting non-independent sources is
Dempster--Shafer theory, decades old. Our cascade is an instance of it with hand-chosen constants,
and we treat that as a limitation: measuring five perturbations of those constants, including
inverting the most- and least-trusted layers, changed the top-10 ranking on 10.6--27.5\% of
samples and the corroborated set on none, because the corroboration decision is a function
of layer count alone.

\subsection{Local deployment}\label{local-deployment}

Confidentiality-driven local deployment is established rather than novel. REx86 \cite{lea2025rex86}
fine-tunes eight open-weight models for x86 reverse engineering explicitly because
``cloud-hosted, closed-weight models pose privacy and security risks and cannot be used in
closed-network facilities'', and validates with a 43-participant user study in which line-level
comprehension improved significantly (p=0.031) while the correct-solve rate rose from 31\% to 53\%
without reaching significance (p=0.189). The capability gap is quantified: the UK AI Security
Institute places open-weight cyber capability 4--7 months behind the closed frontier, narrowing
from 6--10 months, at roughly 45$\times$ lower cost per task.

Position. We inherit this constraint and cite it; we do not claim it. Our deployment
contribution is narrower and practitioner-facing: on a hybrid-offload MoE, KV cache costs
$\approx$10.85 KiB/token and context length barely moves system RAM because the cost is the
offloaded weights, a closed-form estimate over-predicted KV by \textasciitilde4$\times$ and was overturned by
measurement, and speculative decoding gave no throughput gain on this architecture. We found no
comparable deployment-economics reporting for a complete local security pipeline.

\subsection{Evaluation practice}\label{evaluation-practice}

This work's methodological results belong to an established critique tradition rather than
preceding it. Arp et al.'s \emph{Dos and Don'ts} identified ten pitfalls in ML-for-security;
Chasing Shadows \cite{evertz2026chasing} is its direct successor, surveying all 72 peer-reviewed
LLM-security papers from 2023--2024 and finding that every one contains at least one of nine
pitfalls, with only 15.7\% explicitly discussed. Broader benchmark audits reach the same place
from the construct-validity direction across 445 LLM benchmarks.

Position. Our instrument-validity finding, that a single-run parsed-claim count produced
contradictory rankings of the same arms across decoding budgets, is a worked example inside
\cite{evertz2026chasing}'s category, not a new observation, and we present it as such. The nuisance parameter
identified, the inversion measured. Likewise our narrative result. That template-based
generation favours faithfulness while neural generation favours fluency is textbook data-to-text;
what differs is the mechanism. In our paired comparison both arms were perfectly faithful. There were zero hallucinated techniques and precision 1.0, so the entire F1 deficit came from coverage, not
from hallucination. The LLM did not invent; it omitted.

Two evaluation results appear to be unoccupied. First, a case-prior retriever that is genuinely
good in its own vocabulary (F1 0.620 against a 0.424 label-frequency prior) but scores 0.111
against that prior's 0.123 when queried the way production queries it, because query and corpus
share only their boilerplate. Dense retrieval being the standard remedy for vocabulary mismatch
makes this a failure of the remedy, visible only in the retriever's score distribution while
top-k accuracy stayed healthy. A published negative RAG result in malware analysis exists \cite{ng2026evaluating},
by a different mechanism, retrieved context diluting a signal-extraction task, and the
requirement to compare against a trivial label-only baseline is long established \cite{metz2012baselines}, and a
systematic review by the same group found a considerable share of published multi-label results to be
no better than that baseline, with no explanation offered \preprintcite{metz2015comparing}. Second,
an advisory prompt hint whose measurable effect was completion under an operational time
budget rather than mapping accuracy, reducing empty fallback bundles from 6/17 to 1/17; we
found no work measuring generation completion as a channel through which prompt changes act.

Finally, temporal stability, and here the axis matters more than the result. There is a
substantial literature on temporal generalisation in language models, and its finding is
degradation. Performance falls as evaluation moves away from the training period, and scale does
not close the gap \cite{lazaridou2021mind}. That work varies the distance between training time and test time.
Our study varies something else. The model is fixed, and what changes is the era of the
input binary, which is concept drift in the malware-detection sense, applied to an LLM analyst,
rather than temporal misalignment of the model itself.

On that axis we find no LLM-based equivalent, and the axis is worth naming even though we no
longer have a result to report on it. Our n=210 drift study is withdrawn: it drove the full
pipeline per sample against a long-lived shared Ghidra server that was never restarted, which is
the precondition for the stale-program defect described in Section~\ref{sec:failures}, and its per-sample outputs were not
retained, so whether it was affected cannot be determined after the fact. The earlier
earliest-to-latest figure is not restated here. What survives is the framing, input-era drift is a
distinct axis from temporal misalignment, and a re-run under the corrected instrument is required
before any bound is claimed.

\subsection{Silent failure at tool boundaries}\label{silent-failure-at-tool-boundaries}

The paper's methodological spine belongs to an area that already exists, and locating it there
precisely matters more than claiming the ground.

The genus is described, and the description is older than this field. Gunawi et
al.~\cite{gunawi2008eio} measured where an error signal is lost, finding that 13\% of propagation
channels drop an error code without handling it, and calls between modules are a major cause. Yuan
et al.~\cite{yuan2014simple} ruled that a handler which only logs is a handler that ignores, and
attributed 25\% of catastrophic failures to errors that were explicitly signalled and then
ignored. Alquraan et al.~\cite{alquraan2018partition} found the majority of network-partition
failures in cloud systems to be silent, with every warning the rest produced ``confusing, with no
clear mechanism for resolution'', which is the meta-pattern our defects share, stated in an
operating-systems venue a decade before the runtime we built.

Our three boundary defects sit inside that description without strain. An unset optional argument
sent as \texttt{null} and a \texttt{load\_program} that does not change what the server is looking at
are design-assumption mismatches; a refused load answering HTTP 200 is error swallowing in the sense
Yuan et al.~define. The LLM-specific instance of the same shape is now being catalogued too, in bug
taxonomies for agent orchestration frameworks \cite{xue2025agentbugs} and for the inference engines
we run on \cite{liu2026inference}, the latter carrying both \emph{incorrect request parsing} as a
root cause and \emph{silent error} as a symptom.

Adjacent work reaches the same place from the interface side. Mining defects in MCP reference
servers, Oyelayo et al.~\cite{oyelayo2026mcp} find data validation and type issues to be the
second most prevalent class at 13.97\%, the bucket our null-for-unset-optional bug falls into. And
the deeper cause is catalogued: input dependencies that a machine-readable specification cannot
express are ``the norm, rather than the exception, with 85\% of the reviewed APIs'' carrying
them \cite{martinlopez2019deps}. An interface layer unfaithful to its source is a measured quantity
rather than an anecdote.

The detector is not new either. ``Distinct inputs should produce distinct outputs'' is an
ordinary metamorphic relation, and metamorphic testing exists precisely for programs whose correct
output is unknown \cite{li2024metamorphic}, the ``notorious oracle problem'' its own survey names. Stress-testing an
LLM judge's consistency under input variation is likewise established: \cite{dev2026judge} perturbs formatting,
paraphrase, verbosity and ground-truth labels and measures how the judgment moves.

And the newest of our mechanisms is a domain instance, not a discovery. In the course of
building a parameter-size series we produced a rank correlation of +0.866 between model size and
F1, reproducing the $\rho$=0.85 the nearest prior work reports \preprintcite{ryan2026attck}, from five rows that were three models,
one of them present twice at two reasoning settings. The low-scoring row was a model disabled by a
flag rather than limited by its size, and it sat at the small end of the axis where it set the sign.
That phenomenon is established, though more narrowly than we first wrote it. Sun et
al.~\cite{sun2025length} evaluate reasoning under strict output-length constraints and report that
performance ``might not scale monotonically with the model size'': inside a constrained budget
window a smaller model can match or beat a larger one, and the ordinary ordering returns once the
budget relaxes. The inversion is local to the budget regime, not a global reversal, and we
state it that way; the unqualified version is available only from work we could not verify as
reviewed. Their Finding 4 reaches our confound independently. Under the same constraint, reasoning
models do not always beat instruction-tuned ones. A reasoning flag consumes the answer budget, which
makes our disabled arm a brevity-constrained arm under another name.

The general form of the problem is better established still. Mizrahi et al.~\cite{mizrahi2024multiprompt}
find across 6.5M instances that inference-time configuration moves performance ``both absolute and
relative''. The relative half is the ranking, which is what a single-configuration comparison
silently fixes. Budget-dependent model choice runs the same way from the other side, where extra
test-time compute lets a smaller model overtake a much larger one \cite{snell2025testtime,hassid2024budget}.

And the literature does not run one way, which we say because a reviewer will know it.
Jin et al.~\cite{jin2024steplength} find that \emph{lengthening} reasoning steps improves accuracy.
Read together, the two results say the effect is task- and budget-dependent rather than monotone in
either direction, which is our argument rather than an objection to it. A ranking measured at one
output budget does not transfer to another.

What does not appear in this literature, and is what we would add, sits in the remedy rather than the
phenomenon. Their protocol is adapted per model; ours has to be verified per arm, because one of
our providers accepts the parameter that would match two arms and does not act on it, so an arm can
be labelled matched while running the opposite configuration. Selection has to key on the \emph{measured}
reasoning share of a completed arm, never on the flag that was requested. The same is true one layer
down: that OpenAI-compatible servers disagree about which token-limit parameter they honour is
documented in their own issue trackers \preprintcite{llamacpp8634,vllm11976}, so what we contribute there is not the incompatibility
but what one costs inside a measurement, a documented output ceiling absent for as long as it had
existed, and half of a study's calls diverted onto a different artefact-construction path.

What we add is therefore narrow and stated narrowly. Three instances of the described genus,
drawn not from a user-facing assistant but from an evaluation pipeline for security research,
where the consequence is neither a bad answer to a user nor a degraded session but a measurement
that is wrong and looks right. We contribute: the three concrete mechanisms at three different
integration boundaries in MCP argument encoding, server-side current-program state, and HTTP status versus body; the output-cardinality check reported \emph{alongside} the result rather than run as a
test; and a demonstrated cost, one study withdrawn, because its per-sample outputs were not
retained and the question can no longer be asked of it. The last of these is the part we have not
seen elsewhere. The taxonomies report incidents, and we report what an incident of this class does
to a result that has already been written down.

One further claim, and it is about where the genus ends rather than what is in it. The
taxonomies above are organised by the boundary crossed: an error dropped at an inter-module call
\cite{gunawi2008eio}, a signal ignored by its handler \cite{yuan2014simple}, a partition that
produces no actionable warning \cite{alquraan2018partition}. Each describes a call to something
else. Three of our seven mechanisms have no interface in them at all. An ablation that varied the
deterministic reconciliation step and attributed the result to a language model; the correlation
described above; and an output ceiling correct at every level of our own object model and wrong only
in the serialised request. No server misled us in any of the three. They share the meta-pattern, the error signal never reaches a human in actionable form, while sitting outside every class
those taxonomies offer, because each is organised by the boundary crossed and these crossed none. We suggest the organising principle that covers both is not
the boundary but the attribution. The same shape appears wherever a measurement's cause is assigned
rather than measured. We state that as a proposal, on seven cases from one project, not as a
taxonomy.

\subsection{Scope of every empirical statement above}\label{scope-of-every-empirical-statement-above}

Each of our results cited in this section was produced on one model on one machine:
Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at IQ3\_K\_R4 quantisation, served by ik\_llama.cpp \texttt{llama-server} on a single
RTX 5060 / 31 GiB host, with the exact model revision, GGUF digest and engine commit recorded in
the reproducibility appendix. Where a statement concerns retrieval or the deterministic cascade
rather than generation, no model was involved at all and the binding limit is the index and the
corpus instead; those are marked at the point of claim.

We state this here rather than only in Threats to Validity because \cite{evertz2026chasing}'s surrogate fallacy is
the pitfall this work is most exposed to, and because the nearest external evidence sharpens it:
in an ATT\&CK-classification study across models, parameter size was the only statistically
significant predictor of F1 ($\rho$=0.85, p=0.014 \preprintcite{ryan2026attck}) while prompt strategy, chain-of-thought and
temperature were not. Single-model findings are therefore exactly the kind that should not be
read as properties of an architecture. Where a result is a \emph{negative} obtained under a
configuration favourable to the alternative, it travels further than a positive; we say which is
which at each claim.

A second pass on 2026-08-16 checked something the first did not ask: whether each source had
been reviewed at all. It found five entries we had recorded as preprints that are in fact published at SIGIR, ICLR, NeurIPS and an ACM conference, and four author lists wrong, three of them missing
entirely. It also found two figures attributed to papers that do not report them. The rank
correlation between parameter size and ATT\&CK-classification F1 belongs to \preprintcite{ryan2026attck},
whose full text we read to confirm it, not to the multi-hop reasoning study the old numbering
pointed at. The 77.5\% candidate-space reduction belongs to \preprintcite{morbiato2026hierarchical},
not to \cite{lekssays2025techniquerag}, which the registry had credited for it.
Three unreviewed sources were replaced with peer-reviewed work and are recorded above as withdrawals.
Eight remain unreviewed and are marked [preprint] at every use. Each exists to concede
ground rather than claim it, and removing them for want of a venue would have made this paper appear
more original than it is.

The two misattributions have a shape in common, and it is worth naming because it is the one this
paper's own machinery was blind to. Neither number was invented; both are real figures from real
papers. What went wrong was the \emph{binding}, a value recorded against a key that never
reported it, and a checker that asks only ``is this number registered?'' cannot see that. The
registry is now checked in the opposite direction as well. A quoted value must appear in the text
beside the citation it is declared to come from, so a binding that has drifted fails the build
rather than reading as verified.

Acting on \cite{ng2014conll,bryant2019bea}--\cite{dev2026judge} now is conservative because each one \emph{demotes} a claim of ours, the safe
direction. Citing them in a submitted paper on this basis would not be, and the standing rule from
the 2026-08-08 citation audit applies. A search summary is a lead, not a source. That audit exists
because one fabricated a claim.
