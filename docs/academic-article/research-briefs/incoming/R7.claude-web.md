# R7 — Local, small, open-weight models for security analysis

> **Provenance.** Claude (Opus 5), live web search, 2026-08-08, against the current R7 brief.
> Per the brief's carve-out on standing instruction (b), the local-vs-frontier comparison is
> pursued here as the primary axis rather than as an incidental tag.
>
> **Bottom line up front.** Both of the gaps this theme was supposed to own are occupied.
> A systematic open-weight-vs-frontier cyber capability comparison exists and is **quantified**
> — the UK AI Security Institute puts the lag at **4–7 months**, narrowing from 6–10. And
> confidentiality as a *first-class design constraint* is the explicit motivation of a
> peer-reviewed ACSAC 2025 paper (REx86), which also ran the **43-participant user study** we
> do not have.
>
> What survives is narrow but real: nobody reports **deployment economics for a full local
> security pipeline**, and REx86 is a fine-tuned single-shot assistant rather than a tool-driving
> agent — the hole `R1.model-b.md` independently identified.

---

## 1. State of the art

- **REx86: A Local Large Language Model for Assisting in x86 Assembly Reverse Engineering** —
  Darrin Lea, James Ghawaly, Golden Richard III, Aisha Ali-Gombe, Andrew Case.
  **ACSAC 2025** (peer-reviewed). `arXiv:2510.20975`
  - **Confidentiality is the stated motivation, not a bonus:** *"cloud-hosted, closed-weight
    models pose privacy and security risks and cannot be used in closed-network facilities."*
  - Fine-tuned **eight open-weight models** across CodeLlama, Qwen2.5-Coder and CodeGemma on a
    curated dataset of **5,981 x86 assembly examples**; Qwen2.5-Coder-7B was best and became
    REx86. Model, dataset and LoRA adapters **publicly released**.
  - LLM role: assistant over assembly (single-shot), not a tool-driving agent
  - Model tier: **open-weight local throughout**, 7B class
  - Headline: cross-entropy loss **−64.2%**, semantic cosine similarity **+20.3%** over base;
    **user study n=43** — line-level code understanding improved significantly (**p = 0.031**),
    correct-solve rate rose **31% → 53%** but **not significantly (p = 0.189)**
  - Rigor flags: **human study with significance testing — yes** | baseline (base model) — yes |
    artifacts released — yes | CIs? not stated | equal-budget? n/a
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract fetched; venue, numbers and both p-values read directly.
  - **The honest detail most citations of this paper will drop:** the headline solve-rate
    improvement is *not statistically significant*. Whoever cites it should say so; we will.

- **How Far Behind the Frontier are Leading Open Weight Models on Cyber?** — UK **AI Security
  Institute**, blog/report, 2026
  - Measures two regimes: **70 narrow cyber tasks** across four difficulty levels (vulnerability
    research, exploitation, reverse engineering, web exploitation, cryptography), and **cyber
    ranges** measuring end-to-end autonomous attack — one range, "The Last Ones", is a 32-step
    corporate-network attack estimated at **20 hours of human expert time**.
  - Headline: recent open-weight models trail frontier closed models by **4–7 months**, narrower
    than the **6–10 months** measured through 2025. GLM-5.2 (June 2026) ≈ Opus 4.6 / GPT-5.3-Codex
    from ~4 months earlier; DeepSeek V4-Pro ≈ Opus 4.5 from ~5 months earlier.
  - **Cost is reported**, which almost nothing else in this review does: **DeepSeek V4-Pro at
    $0.28 per task versus Opus 4.5 at $12.50** — a ~45× difference.
  - Rigor flags: direct cross-tier comparison — **yes, the only one at scale in this review** |
    two evaluation regimes | cost accounting — yes | CIs? not stated | peer-reviewed? **no** —
    it is an institute publication, not a paper
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on the lag figures, task counts and costs (fetched from the source).
  - **Correction to a search-summary claim:** an earlier search result attributed to this work a
    statement that *"range performance scales log-linearly with token spend up to at least 100
    million tokens per run."* The report as fetched **contains no such discussion of
    inference-time scaling.** The claim is either from a different AISI publication or fabricated
    by the summariser. **Do not cite it.**

- **Local LLM-Based Cyber Incident Analysis in Air-Gapped Networks via Teacher–Student Knowledge
  Distillation and Agentic Orchestration** — *Electronics* (MDPI) 15(13):2949
  - Air-gapped local LLM for incident analysis with agentic orchestration — the closest published
    system to our deployment posture. **Not fetched**; see §5.

- **Mitigating Cyber Risk in the Age of Open-Weight LLMs** — `arXiv:2505.17109`; **The Safety Gap
  Toolkit** — `arXiv:2507.11544`. Both concern the *risk* side of open weights (guardrails
  bypassable when the attacker controls the execution environment) rather than the capability
  side. Relevant to a paper that runs malware through a local model, as a limitation to
  acknowledge. **Neither fetched.**

- **Carried over and directly relevant:**
  - `arXiv:2606.18166` (verified in R2) — seven open-weight models 8B–236B on ATT&CK
    classification; **parameter size the only statistically significant predictor of F1**
    (Spearman ρ = 0.85, p = 0.014); proprietary models deliberately excluded on cost grounds.
  - `arXiv:2604.02460` (verified in R3) — spans Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama **and Gemini
    2.5** in one design, making it the second cross-tier study in the review.
  - From `R1.model-b.md`, unverified by me: **ReCopilot** (`arXiv:2505.16366`, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B),
    **LLM4Decompile** (`arXiv:2403.05286`, 1.3–33B), **Nova** (`arXiv:2311.13721`), **BinMetric**
    (`arXiv:2505.07360`, benchmarking 12 LLMs including open-weight, reporting open-source mean
    22.93% vs closed 27.46%).

### 1b. Rigor tally

Across the 2 individually verified entries:

| property | count | who |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct local-vs-frontier comparison** | **1 / 2** | AISI (and `2604.02460` from R3) |
| **Human study with significance testing** | **1 / 2** | REx86 (n = 43, p reported both ways) |
| **Cost / deployment economics reported** | **1 / 2** | AISI ($0.28 vs $12.50 per task) |
| Artifacts released | **1 / 2** | REx86 (model, dataset, LoRA) |
| Peer-reviewed | **1 / 2** | REx86 (ACSAC 2025) |
| Confidence intervals | **0 / 2** | — |

**Most rigorous, named:** REx86 — peer-reviewed, artifacts released, a human study, and it
reports its *non-significant* result honestly rather than burying it.

### 1c. Model-tier tally — the point of this theme

Counting across everything individually verified in R1–R7 so far:

| | count |
|---|---|
| Studies running **both** tiers in one design | **2** — AISI, `arXiv:2604.02460` |
| Studies open-weight **only**, deliberately | **2** — `arXiv:2606.18166`, REx86 |
| Studies not naming the model at all | **~8** — TechniqueRAG, H-TechniqueRAG, TTPrint, Semantic Ranking, Infer-Retrieve-Rank, AsmRAG, FAX, TTPDetect |

So the field splits into a small, rigorous minority that treats model tier as a variable and a
large majority that does not report it. That majority is the finding: **model tier is not a
reported experimental variable in most of this literature**, which is why the AISI report has
no academic equivalent.

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- ~~**G1 — no systematic local-vs-frontier capability comparison**~~ **CLOSED by prior work.**
  AISI provides one at scale with a quantified lag (4–7 months) and cost figures. Caveat worth
  keeping: AISI measures *offensive* cyber capability, not malware *analysis*, so the transfer to
  our task is an assumption rather than a finding.

- ~~**G2 — confidentiality not treated as a first-class constraint**~~ **CLOSED by prior work.**
  REx86 states it as the reason the work exists, at ACSAC 2025.

- **G3 — Deployment economics for a *full local security pipeline* are unreported.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: AISI reports per-task API cost; REx86 reports model quality. Neither reports what
    it takes to run the whole stack — VRAM, offload strategy, KV growth, throughput under
    co-resident services. Our §2.1 (KV ≈ 10.85 KiB/token; context length barely moves system RAM
    on a hybrid-offload MoE because the cost is the offloaded weights) has no comparator I found.
  - Severity: moderate, and practitioner-facing rather than reviewer-facing.

- **G4 — No peer-reviewed evaluation of a small local model as a *tool-driving agent*.**
  `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: independently identified by `R1.model-b.md`, and consistent with what I found —
    REx86 is single-shot; the agentic systems use frontier models. Its supporting citations are
    unverified by me.
  - Severity: this is the surviving hole, and it belongs to R1 as much as to R7.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Deployment evidence (E2, E3, N8)

- **Prior work claiming similar:** AISI for the capability comparison; REx86 for the
  confidentiality argument. **`NONE FOUND`** for the systems measurements themselves — KV
  scaling on a hybrid-offload MoE, the ~201-tool catalogue infeasibility, or the negative
  speculative-decoding result on an A3B MoE.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.**
  - **The motivation is not ours.** "Samples must not leave the machine" is REx86's stated
    reason for existing, in a peer-reviewed venue, a year earlier. We cite it; we do not claim it.
  - **The measurements may be.** E2 (KV ≈ 10.85 KiB/token, and the correction of a closed-form
    GGUF estimate that over-predicted by ~4×) and N8 (no speculative-decoding gain on A3B MoE)
    are systems facts with no comparator found. They are small, but they are the kind of number
    practitioners cannot get anywhere else.
  - **E3 is the strongest of the three** and its home is R1, not here.

### The comparison we have not run (E.4 / E.8)

- AISI puts the open-weight lag at **4–7 months and narrowing**, and `arXiv:2606.18166` finds
  parameter size is the *only* significant predictor of F1 on ATT&CK classification. Together
  those make our single-model design a live threat to validity — and also make the frontier arm
  *cheaper to justify* than before, because there is now a published expectation to test against.
- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED`, by us.** A frontier arm on the identical pipeline would let us say
  whether our results are about the architecture or about the model. Without it, they are about
  the model until proven otherwise.

### Human evaluation (E.7)

- REx86 ran **n=43** with significance testing and reported an honest null on its headline
  metric. That is the bar. Our E.7 is not just missing, it is missing something a comparable
  paper cleared a year ago — worth knowing before a reviewer says it.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

1. **Reframe local deployment as a constraint we inherit, not a contribution we make.** *Effort:
   trivial.* Cite REx86 for the confidentiality argument and AISI for the capability gap. This
   costs a claim and buys credibility, and both citations are strong.

2. **Publish the systems measurements as a short artifact-facing section.** *Effort: low — the
   numbers exist.* KV scaling on hybrid-offload MoE, the 4× closed-form over-prediction, the
   speculative-decoding null. Nobody else reports these and practitioners need them. Reviewer's
   counter: *"engineering, not research"* — correct, and it belongs in an artifact appendix
   rather than the contributions list.

3. **Run the frontier arm (E.4 / E.8).** *Effort: moderate, cost not risk, `[LLM]`.* Now better
   motivated: AISI predicts a 4–7 month capability lag and `2606.18166` predicts size dominates.
   A result either way is informative, and "we changed only the endpoint" is a clean design.

4. **Borrow REx86's evaluation shape for E.7.** *Effort: high, `[decide]`.* n=43 with pre/post
   task solving and reported p-values. If a human study is ever run, this is the template —
   including the discipline of reporting the non-significant result.

5. **Acknowledge the open-weight risk literature.** *Effort: trivial.* `arXiv:2505.17109` and
   `arXiv:2507.11544` note that guardrails are bypassable when the execution environment is
   attacker-controlled. A system that runs live malware through a local model should say so.
