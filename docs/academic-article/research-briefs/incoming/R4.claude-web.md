# R4 — Grounding, hallucination control, and confidence calibration in LLM security analysis

> **Provenance.** Claude (Opus 5), live web search, 2026-08-08, against the current R4 brief.
> Body citations were resolved by fetching abstract pages; unverified leads are in §5.
>
> **Bottom line up front.** Two findings, pulling in opposite directions.
>
> Against us: *verification-against-tools-before-assertion* has been published
> (`arXiv:2605.27879`, FAX) — in XAI rather than security, and as a claim **filter** rather
> than a confidence **ceiling**, but the idea is out. And abstention / selective prediction is
> a mature field, so "honest degradation" is a system-level instance of it, not a new concept.
>
> For us, and more interesting: `arXiv:2606.29490` finds that an LLM's **verbal confidence
> tracks its readiness to commit, not whether it is right**, while calibrated token
> log-probabilities track correctness. Our entire ISR pipeline runs on model-reported verbal
> confidence — which that paper says is the wrong signal. Our falsification protocol happens to
> be a principled answer to exactly this, because it makes the *permitted* confidence a function
> of tool-executed evidence rather than of what the model says about itself. That reframing is
> worth more than the protocol was worth on its own.

## 1. State of the art

- **Towards Faithful Agentic XAI: A Verification Method and an Open-World Benchmark for Better
  Model Faithfulness** (*FAX*) — Jaechang Kim, Sunung Mun, Seungjoon Lee, Jaewoong Cho,
  Jungseul Ok. `arXiv:2605.27879` (27 May 2026)
  - Decomposes an explanation into **discrete claims**, then *"cross-checks them against
    inherently faithful tools, filtering unsupported or contradictory claims before final
    generation."* Confidence is gated on tool verification.
  - LLM role: explainer, with a deterministic verification layer on top
  - Domain: **XAI over RL/tabular models — not security**
  - Evaluated on: **CRAFTER-XAI-Bench** (new open-world RL benchmark) plus three tabular datasets
  - Headline: simulation faithfulness **0.20 → 0.46** over the strongest baseline, with
    informativeness/relevance/fluency maintained; the authors report explicit verification was
    *essential*
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes | new benchmark contributed | CIs? not stated | stat test? not
    stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence, mechanism and the 0.20→0.46 figure.

- **Reported Confidence in LLMs Tracks Commitment More Than Correctness** — Dharshan Kumaran.
  `arXiv:2606.29490` (28 Jun 2026)
  - **The most important paper in this theme for us.** Two-stage paradigm: the model answers and
    reports confidence, then decides whether to commit or abstain. Verbal confidence predicts
    *commitment*, not correctness; **calibrated token log-probabilities show the opposite
    pattern and track correctness.**
  - Model used: **four non-reasoning and four reasoning models**, plus mechanistic analysis in
    Gemma 3 and 4
  - Headline claim, verbatim: *"verbal confidence is better understood as a behaviour-facing
    readout of an internal commit-readiness state, challenging the practice of treating verbal
    reports as proxies for reliability."*
  - Rigor flags: eight models across two classes | mechanistic analysis | contrastive measure
    (verbal vs log-prob) | CIs? not stated | stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract fetched, claim quoted verbatim.

- **Abstention and selective prediction** — a mature area, not a paper. A model may decline to
  answer where it expects high error, trading coverage for reliability. Recent LLM-facing work
  includes uncertainty-aware abstention with **provable alignment guarantees**
  (`arXiv:2607.04430`), explicit abstention knobs (`arXiv:2601.00138`), prover-verifier
  deliberation for selective prediction (`arXiv:2605.25133`), capability self-assessment
  (`arXiv:2606.00251`), and a decision-theoretic evaluation of LLM confidence
  (`arXiv:2604.03216`).
  - Confidence: **HIGH** that the area is mature and well populated; **LOW** on each individual
    citation — surfaced by search, **none fetched**.

- **Tool-based / computational verification as a general agent pattern** — executing agent
  proposals in controlled environments and observing outcomes (run the code, simulate the
  process, query an authoritative source) rather than trusting model reasoning. Adjacent named
  systems: *E&V* (`arXiv:2312.08477`, pseudo-code execution and verification for static
  analysis), *VeriAct* (`arXiv:2604.00280`), *Agentic Model Checking* (`arXiv:2605.21434`),
  evidence tracing and execution provenance in agents (`arXiv:2606.04990`).
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on the pattern; **LOW** per citation — none fetched.

- **Carried over from other themes and directly relevant here:** *TTPrint*
  (`arXiv:2605.25836`) uses deterministic span localisation and retains only candidates
  supported by **both** localised evidence and the MITRE definition — a
  verification-before-assertion design in the ATT&CK setting. Verified in `R2.claude-web.md`.

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- **G1 — Nobody reports hallucination rates on real malware corpora.** `MEDIUM–HIGH`
  - Evidence: none of the verified work measures on real malware; the security-adjacent
    verification work (TTPrint, FAX) reports task metrics, not hallucination rates. Our own
    ≤0.011 across 210 dated real samples appears to have no comparator.
  - Severity: high — "hallucination" is asserted constantly in this field and rarely quantified
    against real adversarial input.

- **G2 — Calibration of *small local* models for security assertions is unstudied.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: `2606.29490` covers eight models including open-weight ones, but as a general
    property, not for security assertions; and R2's `arXiv:2606.18166` shows parameter size
    dominates in the ATT&CK task, which makes small-model calibration exactly the interesting
    case and exactly the missing one.
  - Severity: moderate, rising with local deployment.

- **G3 — Verbal confidence is used everywhere as a reliability proxy, and one 2026 paper says
  it is not one.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: `2606.29490` explicitly names *"the practice of treating verbal reports as
    proxies for reliability"* as the thing it challenges. That practice is what most multi-agent
    and ISR-style pipelines are built on, including ours.
  - Severity: **high and under-recognised.** If it replicates, a large amount of
    confidence-weighted machinery in this field is weighting noise.

- **G4 — Honest degradation is not a reported output-quality dimension in security.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: abstention is well studied at the *answer* level; I found nothing treating "the
    report declares its own evidence was thin" as a measured property of a security deliverable.
  - Severity: moderate. It is a UX-and-trust property, which is precisely why nobody measures it.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Claim C3 — falsification-before-confidence

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:** **`arXiv:2605.27879` (FAX)** — claim
  decomposition plus cross-checking against inherently faithful tools, filtering unsupported
  claims before generation. Also the general tool-based-verification pattern, and *TTPrint*
  within ATT&CK.
  *How I searched:* the security framing returned little; the hits came from the **XAI** and
  **general-agent-verification** framings. Third consecutive theme where the adjacent field
  owned the answer.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.** Two differences are real and both are stateable in one sentence:
  - **FAX filters; we cap.** FAX drops unsupported claims. Ours keeps the claim and bounds its
    *confidence* — a specific claim (named algorithm, constant/XOR key, hash-resolved API) may
    exceed 0.8 only after active falsification via `emulate_function` or backward
    `analyze_dataflow`, and is otherwise capped at 0.7; high confidence additionally requires
    **≥2 independent evidence loci**. A capped claim survives into the cascade where
    corroboration can still raise it. That is a graded protocol rather than a binary gate.
  - **Domain.** FAX is XAI over RL/tabular models. Ours is binary analysis, where the
    "inherently faithful tool" is an emulator executing the actual function.
  - **But:** C3 is `UNMEASURED` on our side, and FAX reports 0.20 → 0.46 with an ablation
    showing verification is essential. **An unmeasured graded variant of a measured binary
    method is not a contribution.** Measure it or demote it.

### The reframing that `arXiv:2606.29490` hands us

- Not a gap we close — a **justification we did not know we had**. Kumaran finds verbal
  confidence is a commit-readiness readout, not a correctness signal. Our ISR pipeline collects
  exactly that verbal number, and the cascade weights it.
- Our protocol's actual function, restated in those terms: it **stops the model's self-reported
  confidence from being the thing that decides**, by making the permitted ceiling a function of
  *tool-executed evidence*. That is the right structural response to Kumaran's finding, and it
  is a better motivation than the one we currently give (small models are uncalibrated).
- **It also indicts part of our own design.** `LAYER_WEIGHTS` multiplies model-reported claim
  confidences for the three LLM domains. If verbal confidence tracks commitment, those means are
  partly noise — which is consistent with tonight's Layer-0 finding that the weights barely move
  any decision. Two independent routes to "the confidence arithmetic matters less than it looks".

### Claim — honest degradation (the `DEGRADED RUN` banner)

- **Prior work: abstention / selective prediction**, mature, with 2026 LLM work offering
  provable coverage guarantees. Our banner is a *system-level, report-facing* instance.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.** The concept is prior art. What I found no precedent for is
  abstention expressed as a **property of a generated security deliverable** — the artifact
  declaring its own evidence was thin, with reasons enumerated, rather than a model declining to
  answer. Worth one paragraph and a citation to the abstention literature, not a contribution
  section.

### Gap G1 — hallucination on real malware

- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED` by prior work — and we have the number.** ≤0.011 across 210 dated real
  samples, spanning 7 years, with no comparator found in the literature.
- **Caveat that must ship with it:** ours is *invalid/hallucinated technique ID* rate, a narrow
  and mechanically checkable definition. Papers using "hallucination" for unsupported prose are
  measuring something else. State the definition before the number.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

1. **Re-motivate C3 around `arXiv:2606.29490` and then measure it.** *Effort: low to reframe,
   moderate to measure (`[LLM]`).* The ablation is obvious: disable the falsification
   precondition, keep everything else, and measure the invalid-ID rate and the distribution of
   claims above 0.8. FAX's design is the template — they show verification is essential via
   exactly this comparison. Reviewer's counter: *"your protocol is FAX with a threshold"* —
   answered only by showing the graded cap does something a binary filter does not.

2. **Report the hallucination rate as a first-class result with its definition.** *Effort:
   trivial — the number exists.* No comparator exists, so a clearly defined, real-corpus,
   7-year rate is genuinely citable. Reviewer's counter: *"you defined hallucination narrowly"*
   — pre-empt by saying so in the same sentence as the number.

3. **Test whether verbal confidence predicts correctness in our pipeline.** *Effort: moderate,
   `[LLM]`, but partially offline if past ISRs are retained.* Take claims with model-reported
   confidence, score them against ground truth, and check whether the reported number separates
   correct from wrong. If Kumaran replicates here, that is a strong security-domain confirmation
   of a general finding — and it directly justifies every deterministic gate in the system.
   **Cheapest high-value experiment in this theme.**

4. **Pair the degradation banner with the abstention literature rather than claiming it.**
   *Effort: trivial.* One paragraph, one citation, no contribution claim.

5. **Do not claim novelty for tool-based verification.** *Effort: negative.* FAX, TTPrint, E&V
   and the wider agent-verification pattern all occupy it. Our claim is the *graded confidence
   ceiling* and the ≥2-evidence-loci rule, and only if measured.
