# R3 — Multi-agent LLM systems and structured consensus for security analysis

> **Provenance.** Claude (Opus 5), live web search, 2026-08-08, against the current R3 brief.
> Citations in the body were resolved by fetching their abstract pages; unverified leads are in §5.
>
> **Bottom line up front.** This is the most consequential theme so far and the news is mixed.
> The literature's current prior is that **unguided multi-agent debate loses to a single agent
> at equal token budget** — two independent 2026 results, one of them on 7–8B models, i.e. our
> scale. Our "manufactured false corroboration" is a specific instance of an already-named
> failure mode (*sycophantic conformity*, modal adoption up to 85.5%). And weighting evidence
> by source reliability and independence is **Dempster–Shafer theory**, decades old, which makes
> our cascade an ad-hoc instance of a formalism rather than a new idea.
>
> **But the escape hatch is real and both papers name it:** their negative results are about
> *homogeneous* agents decomposing *one* context. Ours are heterogeneous agents on *different
> evidence channels*. That is precisely the condition under which both papers say multi-agent
> can win — which turns E.2 from "an ablation we owe" into **the experiment the paper is about**.

---

## 1. State of the art

- **Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking
  Token Budgets** — Dat Tran, Douwe Kiela (Stanford). `arXiv:2604.02460`
  - Holds the **reasoning-token budget constant** and finds single agents consistently match or
    beat multi-agent systems. Backed by an information-theoretic argument from the **Data
    Processing Inequality**: under a fixed budget with perfect context utilisation, a single
    agent is more information-efficient, because multi-agent decomposition introduces
    communication bottlenecks that lose information.
  - Model used: **three families — Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama, Gemini 2.5** (open-weight
    and cloud)
  - Rigor flags: **equal-budget control — yes, it is the paper's entire point** | multiple model
    families | CIs? not stated | stat test? not stated
  - **The caveat that matters to us — and it is MEASURED, not argued (checked in the full text,
    2026-08-08).** §3.1 states the theory: *"once effective single-agent context utilization
    deteriorates sufficiently, a well-designed MAS may recover task-relevant information more
    reliably than a degraded single pass."* §5.3 then runs controlled degradation experiments
    across four methods (deletion, masking, substitution, distractor insertion) and finds a
    **crossover**: *"Under masking, SAS leads at mild degradation (α=0.3) and the systems are
    roughly tied at moderate degradation, but Sequential becomes better at heavy degradation
    (α=0.7)"*, with the same pattern under substitution.
    **And the degradation experiment ran on Qwen3-30B-A3B** — the same A3B MoE class we deploy.
    So our defence is not a hopeful reading of a discussion section; it is an experimentally
    established crossover with a named location, on our model class, and E.2's job is to find
    out which side of it a real malware pipeline sits on.
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence, authors, claim and the caveat (fetched via a paper
    overview page); **MEDIUM** on exact numbers — I did not read the PDF.

- **The Cost of Consensus: Isolated Self-Correction Prevails Over Unguided Homogeneous
  Multi-Agent Debate** — Blaž Bertalanič, Carolina Fortuna. `arXiv:2605.00914` (29 Apr 2026)
  - Teams of **10 homogeneous agents** debating, versus isolated self-correction, versus a
    stochastic-noise control (injecting unrelated rationales — a control design worth stealing).
  - Model used: **Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-3-8B** — all open-weight, all at
    **roughly our deployment scale**
  - Headline: debate consumes **2.1–3.4× more tokens** (up to 28,631 per problem) *"for equal or
    lower accuracy"*
  - Three named failure modes, all directly relevant:
    - **sycophantic conformity** — agents uncritically adopt majority answers, **modal adoption
      up to 85.5%**
    - **contextual fragility** — vulnerability rate up to **70.0%**
    - **consensus collapse** — plurality voting discards correct answers already present in the
      pool, **oracle gap up to 32.3 percentage points**
  - Rigor flags: **token-budget accounting — yes** | **a noise control arm — yes, rare** |
    three model families | CIs? not stated | stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract fetched, all four numbers quoted verbatim.
  - **Scope limit stated by the authors:** *homogeneous teams without structured roles*.

- **Dempster–Shafer evidence theory and multi-sensor fusion** — a mature formalism, not a paper.
  Combining evidence from multiple sources with **explicit reliability weights** is standard:
  static and dynamic source reliability, evidence-distance and belief-entropy weighting,
  IOWA operators for conflicting sources, and — the part that matters most here — **similarity
  weighting via Pearson correlation between sources, precisely to discount evidence that is not
  independent**. Representative venues: *Sensors* (MDPI) 23(11):5141; *Int. J. Intelligent
  Systems* 35(8) (Koksalmis & Kabak, `10.1002/int.22237`); several PMC-indexed fault-diagnosis
  papers.
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on the formalism and that source-independence weighting is part of it;
    **LOW** on any single citation — I read search summaries, not the papers.

- **PhishDebate** — `arXiv:2506.15656`. Structured multi-agent debate for phishing-website
  detection, emphasising modularity and explainability. Surfaced by `ALL8.model-a.md`.
  **Not fetched** — see §5. The closest *security* application of structured debate I found.

- **Voting or Consensus? Decision-Making in Multi-Agent Debate** — surfaced by
  `ALL8.model-a.md` via a ResearchGate link. Compares decision protocols (majority voting,
  unanimity). **Not fetched, no arXiv ID resolved** — see §5.

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- **G1 — The equal-budget question is answered, and the answer is against multi-agent.** `HIGH`
  - Not a gap any more. Two independent 2026 results, one at 7–8B. Our brief asked "is there
    work quantifying this?" — there is, and it says no.
  - Consequence for us: E.2 is no longer an ablation we merely owe. The **prior is negative**,
    so a positive result would be a genuine finding and a negative one would be a replication.

- **G2 — Sycophancy between agents is measured, but not in a security setting.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: `2605.00914` quantifies modal adoption at up to 85.5% on reasoning benchmarks.
    I found no equivalent measurement where the agents hold *different evidence* and the
    downstream consumer is a corroboration counter.
  - Severity: moderate-to-high — in a security pipeline the echo is not just a wrong answer, it
    is a *fabricated independent confirmation*, which is a different and worse failure.

- **G3 — Nobody weights LLM agents by evidence-channel independence.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: multi-agent work weights by model-reported confidence or by vote. The
    independence-weighting machinery exists in Dempster–Shafer fusion but I found no work
    importing it into LLM multi-agent consensus.
  - Severity: this is the most promising *unoccupied* position in the theme.

- **G4 — The "agent with no data still speaks" failure mode is unnamed.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: `2605.00914`'s failure modes are about majority pressure among agents that all
    have the same information. An agent whose *evidence channel is empty* and which therefore
    paraphrases its peers is a distinct mechanism. I found no name for it.
  - Severity: high in any heterogeneous-evidence architecture, which is most security pipelines.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Claim C6 — cascade weighting by which independent evidence layers corroborate

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution: Dempster–Shafer evidence theory**, including
  explicit source-reliability weights and correlation-based discounting of non-independent
  sources. Decades of it.
  *How I searched:* the security framing returned nothing; the **classical-fusion** framing
  ("weighting evidence sources by independence and reliability", "sensor fusion", "corroboration
  from multiple detectors") returned the formalism immediately. Another instance of the R2
  lesson.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`, and weaker than we assumed.**
  - The *idea* — weight sources by trustworthiness, reward corroboration across independent
    sources — is classical. `LAYER_WEIGHTS` 0.90…0.20 and multipliers 1.00…1.90 are an ad-hoc
    instantiation of it, with hand-picked constants and no derivation.
  - What is genuinely unoccupied (G3): applying it to **LLM agents**, where the "sensors" are a
    mix of deterministic rule engines and probabilistic models, and where the reliability
    ordering is the design claim.
  - **This makes E.1 worse, not better.** A reviewer who knows D–S will ask why we did not use
    it — or at minimum why our constants beat a principled alternative. "Unmeasured constants"
    was already the weakest point; "unmeasured constants where a formalism exists" is weaker.
  - **Concrete opportunity:** express the cascade in D–S terms, or measure ours against a D–S
    baseline. That converts the weakest claim into a positioned one.

### Claim — manufactured false corroboration (the dataless-analyst echo)

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution/finding:** `arXiv:2605.00914` names **sycophantic
  conformity** and measures it (modal adoption up to 85.5%) on 7–8B models. Our live
  observation — the sycophancy detector firing at `sim=1.000` — is the same family.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.** The *phenomenon* is documented. What is not:
  - our mechanism is **an empty evidence channel**, not majority pressure. The agent does not
    conform because peers outnumber it; it speaks because it has nothing and its peers' text is
    the only input;
  - the *consequence* is domain-specific and worse: the echo returns tagged with the silent
    agent's own domain, and a corroboration counter reads
    `len(contributing_layers) >= 2` as independent confirmation. A wrong answer becomes a
    **corroborated** wrong answer, and `LAYER_WEIGHTS["dynamic"]=0.45` outranks the static
    source it was copied from.
  - **This is a defensible contribution** provided it is framed as a heterogeneous-evidence
    instance of a known conformity failure, with the corroboration-counter consequence as the
    novel part — not as the discovery of agent sycophancy.

### Gap G1 — does the negotiation earn its cost?

- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED`, and now under active threat.**
  - The prior in the literature is that it does not, established twice in 2026, once on models
    our size.
  - Our defence is structural and is named by both papers: their negative results concern
    **homogeneous** agents decomposing a **single** context. Ours are heterogeneous agents on
    **different evidence channels** — static PE, sandbox behaviour, network — which is closer to
    Tran & Kiela's stated exception ("when a single agent's effective context utilisation is
    degraded by long or noisy contexts"). A 600-import PE plus a sandbox report plus PCAP is
    exactly a long, noisy context.
  - **So E.2 is no longer housekeeping. It is the paper's central experiment**, and it has a
    pre-registered hypothesis worth stating: *heterogeneous evidence-channel decomposition
    survives an equal-budget control where homogeneous debate does not.* If it fails, that is
    still publishable — and honest.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

1. **Run E.2 to Bertalanič & Fortuna's design, not a design of our own.** *Effort: moderate,
   `[LLM]`.* Three arms: negotiated multi-agent, single agent given all evidence, and a
   **noise-control arm** (feed one analyst unrelated evidence). Equal total tokens, N≫1, CIs.
   Report token cost explicitly — the comparison the field now expects. What would close it: a
   positive delta at equal budget, or an honest negative that replicates theirs in a
   heterogeneous setting. Reviewer's counter: *"you only show it on your own pipeline"* —
   unavoidable, so report the mechanism (which channel contributed what), not just the score.

2. **Measure the corroboration counter under a silent channel.** *Effort: low, `[LLM]` for the
   full loop but the failure is reproducible deterministically.* Inject an empty evidence
   channel and show how often a single-source finding is promoted to CORROBORATED with the fix
   off, and that it is zero with the fix on. This makes the pathology a *measurement* rather
   than an anecdote — currently our strongest R3 material and it rests on one live observation.

3. **Position the cascade against Dempster–Shafer.** *Effort: low for the positioning,
   moderate for a baseline arm, `[cheap]` for the arithmetic.* Either derive the weights or
   compare against a D–S combination on the same evidence tuples. The static Layer-0 study
   already planned for tonight produces exactly the tuples this needs. Reviewer's counter:
   *"why not just use D–S?"* — which we should be able to answer with a number.

4. **Name the failure mode.** *Effort: trivial, value: disproportionate.* "Manufactured
   corroboration from a silent evidence channel" is a nameable, transferable pathology for
   heterogeneous multi-agent systems. Names travel further than architectures.

5. **Do not claim multi-agent superiority anywhere in the paper until (1) is run.** *Effort:
   negative.* The field now has two equal-budget negative results; an unsupported claim is a
   desk-reject risk, and our own N1 says the same thing in our own words.
