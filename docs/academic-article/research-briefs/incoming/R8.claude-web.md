# R8 — Automated CTI report generation and machine-readable output quality

> **Provenance.** Claude (Opus 5), live web search, 2026-08-08, against the current R8 brief.
> Body citations resolved by fetching; unverified leads in §5.
>
> **Bottom line up front.** Our N5 — the deterministic template beating the LLM narrative on
> faithfulness and coverage, with readability as the LLM's only edge — is a **security-domain
> replication of a well-established NLG trade-off**. Template-based generation prioritising
> factual accuracy at the cost of fluency, versus neural generation that is fluent and prone to
> hallucination, is textbook data-to-text. Publishable as a domain replication with numbers;
> not as a discovery.
>
> STIX validity is also already measured: `eLLM-CTI` introduces a **STIX accuracy metric** for
> valid and complete bundles. Our C7 is adjacent but distinct — it is a deterministic **repair**
> pass, not a metric — and that distinction is the whole of what is left to claim.
>
> The genuinely unoccupied finding is C8: an advisory prompt hint whose real effect was
> **completion under a time budget**, invisible to any accuracy metric.

## 1. State of the art

- **Enhanced-LLM extraction of CTI from unstructured threat reports** (*eLLM-CTI*) —
  *Future Generation Computer Systems*, `DOI 10.1016/j.future.2026.108...` (ScienceDirect
  `S0167739X26001482`)
  - Extracts threat information from unstructured reports into **STIX 2.1**, using a reasoning
    LLM (**GPT-OSS:20B — open-weight**) plus RAG over a security knowledge base.
  - **Introduces a "STIX accuracy" metric** to assess how accurately a model generates *valid
    and complete* structured STIX bundles — i.e. output validity is already a measured
    dimension in this subfield.
  - LLM role: extractor/generator; RAG as grounding
  - Model tier: **open-weight, 20B**
  - Headline: RAG improves identification of STIX Domain Objects and correlation into STIX
    Relationship Objects; aims at feeds deployable without further analyst processing
  - Rigor flags: new metric contributed | baseline (with/without RAG) — yes | CIs? unknown |
    stat test? unknown
  - Confidence: **MEDIUM–HIGH** on existence, venue and the STIX-accuracy metric (consistent
    across two independent search sources); **LOW** on numbers — the publisher page is
    paywalled and I did **not** fetch the full text.

- **A Structured Cyber Threat Intelligence Dataset Using STIX 2.1 Entities and MITRE ATT&CK
  Mappings** — Dipshikha Das, Arnab Banik, Md. Shariful Islam, Md Rayhanur Rahman.
  `arXiv:2607.23312` (25 Jul 2026)
  - **150 English CTI reports as STIX 2.1 graphs: 4,777 STIX entities, 5,817 relationships,
    1,273 attack-pattern entities mapped to 269 unique ATT&CK Enterprise techniques and
    sub-techniques.**
  - **Four locally deployed open-source LLMs** evaluated for annotation. **Qwen3.6:27B was
    strongest** — kappa up to **0.803**, micro-F1 **>92%**, false-positive rate **<5%**.
  - Rigor flags: **inter-annotator kappa — yes** | multiple models | explicit FPR | CIs? not
    stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and all quoted numbers (abstract fetched). **Release
    status not stated in the abstract** — see §5.
  - **Two reasons this matters to us specifically.** It is the same model family we run in
    production, evaluated properly and performing well at annotation — a useful external data
    point for R7. And it is a STIX-2.1-plus-ATT&CK corpus, which is close to a benchmark we
    could be measured against.

- **Large Language Models Are Unreliable for Cyber Threat Intelligence** — `arXiv:2503.23175`.
  Title alone makes it required reading for a paper making CTI-quality claims. **Not fetched**
  — see §5.

- **Template-based vs neural data-to-text generation** — a mature NLG literature, not a paper.
  The consistent finding: *early rule-based and template-based NLG systems were designed to
  prioritise factual accuracy, often at the cost of linguistic fluency*, while *encoder-decoder
  models generate fluent text but are prone to hallucination — fluent but unfaithful to the
  source*. Representative anchors surfaced: confident decoding for faithful data-to-text
  (`arXiv:1910.08684`), content-matching constraints for faithful table-to-text, hallucination
  and predictive uncertainty in conditional generation (`arXiv:2103.15025`).
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on the trade-off being established; **LOW** per anchor — **none
    fetched**.

- **Tooling and standards** — the OASIS **cti-stix-validator** checks JSON-schema conformance
  *plus* requirements not expressible in schema, plus best practices. So a validity check is
  available off the shelf, which raises rather than lowers the bar for claiming validity work.

- **Also surfaced, unverified:** *STIXAgent* (multi-agent framework for standardised CTI report
  management), *TIEF* (MDPI, TTP extraction framework), IoC-extraction work
  (`arXiv:2506.11325`), SOC workflow automation (`arXiv:2407.13093`).

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- ~~**G1 — STIX spec-validity is never checked**~~ **CLOSED by prior work.** eLLM-CTI measures
  it; the OASIS validator exists. What is *not* addressed is **referential integrity repair** —
  see §3.

- **G2 — No deterministic-template baseline for LLM CTI narratives.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: none of the verified CTI-generation work compares against a template. The NLG
    literature has the trade-off in general form, but I found no instance of it being tested in
    security reporting, where the cost of an unfaithful sentence is different.
  - Severity: moderate — and it is exactly the experiment we ran.

- **G3 — Generation completion under an operational budget is unmeasured.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: everything found reports accuracy/coverage metrics. I found no work measuring how
    often generation *fails to finish* within a deployment's time budget, or treating that as
    the channel through which a prompt change acts.
  - Severity: moderate for research, high for practice — and invisible to every metric in §1.

- **G4 — Honest degradation is not a reported quality dimension.** `MEDIUM`
  - Same finding as R4's G4, from the output side. A report that declares its own evidence was
    thin is not something anyone measures.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Claim N5 — the deterministic template beats the LLM narrative on faithfulness + coverage

- **Prior work claiming a similar finding: the data-to-text NLG literature**, in general form —
  templates are faithful and stilted, neural output is fluent and hallucination-prone.
  *How I searched:* CTI framing first (little), then the **NLG / data-to-text** framing, which
  returned the trade-off immediately. Fourth theme where the adjacent field owned the answer.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.** The *trade-off* is textbook. What I found no instance of:
  - the comparison run **in a security-reporting setting** with a paired design, bootstrap CIs
    and a sign test (F1 delta −0.111, CI excluding 0, fallback wins 7 / ties 8 / LLM wins 0);
  - the result that **both arms were perfectly faithful** (precision 1.0, zero hallucinated
    techniques) so the delta came entirely from *coverage*, not from hallucination. That is a
    sharper statement than the general trade-off, and it partly contradicts it: our LLM did not
    hallucinate, it *omitted*.
  - Framing to use: *"the classic template-vs-neural trade-off holds in security reporting, but
    the mechanism differs — the loss is coverage, not faithfulness."*

### Claim C7 — deterministic STIX integrity + cascade reconciliation

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:** eLLM-CTI **measures** STIX validity; the OASIS
  validator **checks** conformance.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`, and narrower than we assumed.** Validity as a *dimension* is taken.
  What I found no precedent for is the **repair** pass: dropping empty-pattern indicators,
  deduplicating attack-patterns by technique ID while **rewriting relationship refs to the
  survivor**, sweeping relationships whose endpoints no longer resolve, trimming dangling
  `object_refs`, and — the part with a measured motivation — **reconciling the bundle against
  the cascade** so the emitted STIX carries what the report says it found (5 attack-patterns
  emitted vs 39 techniques in `ttp_mappings`, measured 2026-08-07).
- **The honest problem: C7 is `UNMEASURED`.** A validator exists off the shelf; "we repair
  bundles" is a design claim until we report how often, and what breaks without it. The
  measurement is **cheap and offline** — run the OASIS validator over bundles with the pass on
  and off. It needs stored bundles, which the LLM path produces, so it is `[LLM]` only to
  generate inputs.

### Claim C8 — the hint's real effect was completion, not accuracy

- **Prior work: `NONE FOUND`.** No work measures generation completion under an operational
  budget, or identifies it as the channel through which an advisory prompt change acts.
- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED` — this is the strongest surviving claim in the theme.**
  Empty/fallback bundles **6/17 → 1/17**, with the exact-F1 gain small and its CI including 0.
  The general lesson transfers well beyond CTI: *an "advisory, low-impact" prompt addition can
  have a first-order effect through an unmeasured channel, and mapping-quality metrics cannot
  see it.*
- Caveat that must ship with it: this is an **operational, timeout-mediated** effect specific to
  a slow local model under a fixed 600 s ceiling. With a faster model the gap shrinks. Say so.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

1. **Lead this theme with C8, not N5.** *Effort: trivial (framing).* C8 has no prior art found;
   N5 is a domain replication. Reviewer's counter to C8: *"an artifact of your timeout"* — which
   is true and should be stated first, then defended as a real property of local deployment.

2. **Measure C7 with the OASIS validator.** *Effort: low, `[cheap]` once bundles exist.*
   Integrity pass on vs off, scored by an *external, standard* validator rather than our own
   checks. Turns a design description into a number and uses someone else's instrument — which
   is exactly what our own N1 says to do. Reviewer's counter: *"the validator already exists, so
   just run it in production"* — answerable only by showing what the repair recovers that
   rejection alone would discard.

3. **Reframe N5 around the mechanism.** *Effort: trivial.* Not "templates beat LLMs" but "the
   template-vs-neural trade-off holds here, and the LLM's loss is coverage rather than
   faithfulness — both arms hallucinated nothing." That is a finer-grained claim than the NLG
   literature's, and it is ours.

4. **Check `arXiv:2607.23312` for release, and evaluate against it if public.** *Effort:
   moderate.* 150 reports, 4,777 entities, 269 techniques, annotated with kappa — and its best
   annotator is our own model family. Third theme in which an external corpus check appears as
   the top practical action.

5. **Read `arXiv:2503.23175` before making any CTI-quality claim.** *Effort: trivial.* A paper
   titled *Large Language Models Are Unreliable for Cyber Threat Intelligence* either supports
   our negative results or contradicts our positive ones. Either way we must have read it.
