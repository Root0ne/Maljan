## 1. State of the art

- **SoK: Automated TTP Extraction from CTI Reports – Are We There Yet?** — Büchel, Paladini,
  Longari, Carminati, Zanero, Binyamini, Engelberg, Klein, Guizzardi, Caselli, Continella,
  van Steen, Peter, van Ede. **USENIX Security Symposium 2025**, pp. 4621–4641,
  ISBN 978-1-939133-52-6.
  - Systematises 40+ papers spanning NER, embedder models and generative LLMs, then
    **empirically re-evaluates them in one unified setting** — the thing nobody else did.
  - LLM role: surveys all roles; itself evaluative
  - Model used: multiple across the surveyed corpus; the SoK's own re-evaluation covers
    traditional NLP, embedder and generative approaches
  - Evaluated on: a unified re-run of existing approaches; the paper's premise is that prior
    datasets are "custom, inaccessible"
  - Headline result, verbatim: *"(1) the finding of a kind of performance limit that existing
    approaches seemingly cannot overcome as of yet, (2) that traditional NLP approaches
    (possibly counterintuitively) outperform modern embedder-based and generative approaches in
    realistic settings, and (3) that further research on understanding inherent ambiguities in
    TTP ontologies and on the creation of qualitative datasets is key"*
  - Rigor flags: CIs? not stated | repeats? n/a | baseline? **unified re-evaluation of 40+
    prior systems — the strongest control in this field** | equal-budget? n/a | stat test? not
    stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract retrieved verbatim from two independent institutional
    repositories (Twente, Continella's publication page); page range and ISBN consistent.

- **TechniqueRAG: Retrieval Augmented Generation for Adversarial Technique Annotation in Cyber
  Threat Intelligence Text** — Lekssays, Shukla, Sencar, Parvez. **ACL Findings 2025**.
  `arXiv:2505.11988`
  - A domain RAG framework: off-the-shelf retrievers for candidate extraction, instruction-tuned
    LLMs to **re-rank** candidate (sub-)techniques, and minimal text–technique pairs used only to
    fine-tune the generator. Explicitly motivated by data scarcity: ATT&CK defines 550+
    (sub-)techniques but only ~10,000 annotated examples exist publicly.
  - LLM role: re-ranker / generator over retrieved candidates
  - Model used: "instruction-tuned LLMs" — **not named in the abstract**
  - Evaluated on: not specified in the abstract
  - Headline result: claims SOTA "without extensive task-specific optimizations or labeled data";
    numbers not in the abstract
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes (prior SOTA) | others not determinable from the abstract
  - Confidence: **HIGH** that the paper exists and is ACL Findings 2025; **LOW** on its numbers —
    I did not read the full text.

- **Hierarchical Retrieval Augmented Generation for Adversarial Technique Annotation** (H-TechniqueRAG)
  — Morbiato, Keller, Nair, Romano. `arXiv:2604.14166` (Mar 2026)
  - Injects the **tactic→technique taxonomy as an inductive bias**: filter at tactic level first,
    then search techniques within those tactics. Reduces the candidate space by **77.5%**.
  - LLM role: generator over hierarchically narrowed candidates
  - Model used: **not named in the abstract** (open vs cloud undetermined)
  - Evaluated on: "three diverse CTI datasets"
  - Headline result: **+3.8% F1** over TechniqueRAG, **−62.4% inference latency**, **−60% LLM API
    calls**
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes (TechniqueRAG) | CIs? no | repeats? not stated | equal-budget? no |
    stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and headline numbers (abstract fetched); **MEDIUM** on
    everything requiring the full text.

- **Evaluating Open-Source LLMs for Multi-Label ATT&CK Technique Classification on CTI Reports**
  — Ahmed Ryan et al. `arXiv:2606.18166` (Jun 2026)
  - The single most useful paper for our purposes. **53 configurations across 7 open-weight
    models**: DeepSeek-V2.5 (236B), GPT-OSS-120B, Llama 3.1 Instruct (70B), Gemma 3 (27B),
    GPT-OSS-20B, Gemma 3 (12B), Llama 3.1 Instruct (8B).
  - LLM role: classifier
  - Model used: **open-weight only, 8B–236B — proprietary models deliberately excluded**
    ("to avoid the expense of proprietary LLMs")
  - Evaluated on: 2,076 human-annotated sentences (1,281 technique-positive, 795 negative) from
    83 unstructured CTI reports, 114 unique techniques; inter-annotator **κ = 0.68**. The paper
    argues prior evaluations used "simplified, single-technique sentences" that **inflated**
    results.
  - Headline result: best non-RAG **micro-F1 = 0.22** (DeepSeek-V2.5, T=0, 3-shot, CoT);
    **RAG pipeline = 0.32** (Llama-70B), +0.10 over the best baseline
  - Rigor flags: CIs? no | repeats? 53 configs | baseline? yes (non-RAG vs RAG) | equal-budget?
    no | **stat test? YES — Spearman ρ; parameter size ρ=0.85, p=0.014; few-shot, CoT and
    temperature not significant**
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract and full-text HTML both fetched; model list and numbers read
    directly.

- **TTPrint: Evidence-Grounded TTP Extraction via Diverge-then-Converge Verification** —
  Cheng, Li, Basuki, Cui, Ding, Gao. `arXiv:2605.25836` (May 2026)
  - Two phases mirroring analyst workflow: **divergent** (decompose the report into atomic
    behaviours, extract broad technique candidates) then **convergent** (deterministic span
    localisation anchors candidates to evidence windows; a verification step retains only
    candidates supported by *both* the localised evidence *and* the MITRE definition).
  - LLM role: analyst/extractor, with a deterministic verification layer on top
  - Model used: six LLMs — **not named in the abstract**
  - Evaluated on: **TRAM-Clean** (a cleaned version of the existing benchmark) and
    **TTPrint-Bench** (new document-level benchmark, 150 full CTI reports, 12 sources, 2022–2025,
    125 techniques incl. 66 rare ones for long-tail evaluation)
  - Headline result: **76.48% macro-F1** on TRAM-Clean (+63.5% over baseline); **87.39% macro-F1**
    on TTPrint-Bench (+29.4%)
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes | **new + cleaned benchmarks — a methodological contribution** |
    CIs? not stated | repeats? not stated | stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and headline numbers; **MEDIUM** on method detail.

- **Semantic Ranking for Automated Adversarial Technique Annotation in Security Text** —
  `arXiv:2403.17068`
  - Frames annotation as **ranking, not generation**: a multi-stage ranking architecture
    "jointly optimizing for efficiency and effectiveness".
  - LLM role: ranker; compared against zero-shot LLMs
  - Model used: not named in the abstract
  - Evaluated on: 193 top-level techniques
  - Headline result: **top-3 recall 81%**; **+40% over widely used LLMs zero-shot**
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes (zero-shot LLMs) | ranking metric only — **no threshold/gate
    metric reported** | CIs? no | stat test? no
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and the two numbers; **MEDIUM** on architecture detail.

- **In-Context Learning for Extreme Multi-Label Classification** (*Infer-Retrieve-Rank*) —
  D'Oosterlinck, Khattab, Remy, Demeester, Develder, Potts. `arXiv:2401.12178`
  - **Not a security paper — and that is why it matters.** A general DSPy program for label
    spaces with thousands of classes that defines "multi-step interactions between LMs and
    retrievers", decoupling the LM's inference from direct assignment over the label space: the
    retriever surfaces candidates, the LM ranks.
  - LLM role: inferencer + ranker, never a label-space recaller
  - Evaluated on: three extreme-multi-label benchmarks
  - Headline result: **SOTA on three benchmarks using only tens of labeled examples and no
    finetuning**
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes | CIs? not stated | stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract fetched, method description confirmed verbatim.

- **AnnoCTR: A Dataset for Detecting and Linking Entities, Tactics, and Techniques in Cyber
  Threat Reports** — Lange et al. **LREC-COLING 2024**. `arXiv:2404.07765`
  - Expert-annotated CTI corpus linking entities and concepts to Wikipedia and ATT&CK, capturing
    **implicitly** mentioned techniques, annotated at whole-document granularity rather than
    "single labels per document or decontextualized sentences". CC-BY-SA, on GitHub.
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and scheme; **MEDIUM** on size (not in abstract).

- **Identifying Adversary Tactics and Techniques in Malware Binaries with an LLM Agent**
  (*TTPDetect*) — Purdue University. `arXiv:2602.06325` (submitted 6 Feb 2026).
  **Added by correction — the most consequential paper in this review for us.**
  - Self-described as **"the first LLM agent for recognizing TTPs in stripped malware
    binaries"**. Motivated by exactly our setting: real binaries are stripped, contain many
    functions, and spread malicious behaviour across code regions. Architecture: dense
    retrieval + LLM-based neural retrieval narrows analysis **entry points** (deterministic
    pre-pass), then a function-level agent with a *Context Explorer* doing on-demand
    incremental context retrieval and a *TTP Reasoner* with inference-time reasoning
    guidelines.
  - LLM role: analyst over decompiled functions, on top of a deterministic retrieval pre-pass
  - Model used: **not named in the abstract**; the second incoming report states example
    outputs use Claude 3.7 Sonnet (cloud) — unverified by me
  - Evaluated on: **a new dataset labelling decompiled functions with TTPs** across diverse
    malware families and platforms (size not in the abstract), plus real-world malware and
    expert-written reports
  - Headline result: **93.25% precision / 93.81% recall** function-level (+10.38 / +18.78 over
    baselines); **87.37% precision** on real-world malware; recovers **85.7%** of
    expert-documented techniques and surfaces on average **10.5 previously unreported TTPs**
    per sample
  - Rigor flags: baseline? yes | new labelled dataset — a real contribution | CIs? not stated |
    repeats? not stated | equal-budget? no | stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence, abstract and headline numbers (fetched twice,
    independently); **MEDIUM** on architecture detail; **LOW** on model tier.

- **Classical baselines named by the SoK and by the papers above** — TRAM (MITRE; logistic
  regression), TRAM2 (50 most frequent techniques), rcATT (multi-label → tactics+techniques,
  STIX output), TTPDrill (reported >82% precision/recall), EXTRACTOR, AttacKG, AttacKG+, LADDER,
  MITREtrieval, CTINexus. Confidence **MEDIUM** — these are consistently referenced across the
  verified sources above, but I did not fetch each original paper.

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- **G1 — No direct local-vs-frontier comparison for ATT&CK mapping.** `HIGH`
  - Evidence: the only large open-weight study (`2606.18166`) explicitly excluded proprietary
    models on cost grounds; no verified paper runs both tiers on the same task.
  - Severity: the field cares — `2606.18166` found parameter size is the *only* significant
    predictor (ρ=0.85, p=0.014), which makes the missing upper anchor conspicuous.

- **G2 — Ranking quality and threshold/gating quality are not reported as distinct axes.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: Semantic Ranking reports top-3 recall only; TechniqueRAG and H-TechniqueRAG report
    F1/latency; TTPrint has a verification stage but reports macro-F1, not a separation metric for
    the verifier itself. None reports "how well does the score separate correct from wrong picks".
  - Severity: moderate but rising — every RAG-style system now has an implicit accept/reject step
    whose quality is invisible in the headline metric.

- **G3 — Auto-correction of model-assigned technique IDs, and its regressions, is unstudied.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: the closest work, LlmCorr (`arXiv:2402.13414`, unverified here), runs the *opposite*
    direction — an LLM correcting an ML model's predictions. I found nothing measuring the cost of
    a deterministic layer overriding an LLM's already-valid label.
  - Severity: low visibility, high practical importance for anyone shipping such a layer.

- **G4 — The degenerate label-recall loop as a small-model pathology is undocumented.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: hallucinated-ID *concern* is well represented (TTPrint's verification stage;
    constrained decoding as the standard remedy), but I found no paper documenting a model
    burning its entire budget enumerating candidate IDs, nor any measuring that sampler
    penalties are insufficient against it.
  - Severity: genuinely under-explored; matters specifically at small model scale, which the
    field barely studies (G1).

- ~~**G5 — Benchmarks measure sentence-level CTI prose; nothing maps *binary* evidence.** `HIGH`~~
  **RETRACTED — this is not a gap.** `arXiv:2602.06325` (TTPDetect) maps stripped malware
  binaries to ATT&CK at function level and builds a decompiled-function↔TTP dataset to do it.
  What survives is narrower and must be stated carefully:
  - **G5′ — the *benchmarks* remain prose-only even though systems have moved on.** `MEDIUM`
    TRAM/TRAM2, TTPrint-Bench and AnnoCTR are all report-text corpora; TTPDetect had to build
    its own labelled set rather than use a shared one. So there is still no *common* benchmark
    for binary→ATT&CK, which is why TTPDetect's numbers (93.25% precision) and the SoK's
    prose-side ceiling cannot be placed on the same axis.
  - Severity: moderate. It is a comparability problem, not an unexplored problem — and it is a
    specific instance of the SoK's own insight (3).

- **G6 — Ontological ambiguity and dataset quality are the field's self-declared blockers.** `HIGH`
  - Evidence: the SoK names them as insight (3) — this is not my inference but the SoK's own
    stated conclusion.
  - Severity: highest — a SoK at USENIX Security saying "better datasets are the way forward" is
    the field's consensus statement of its own gap.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Claim C5 — "describe-then-map": remove ID recall from the model, let deterministic retrieval assign the ID

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:**
  **`arXiv:2401.12178` (Infer-Retrieve-Rank, D'Oosterlinck et al.)** — a general program that
  decouples LM inference from assignment over a many-thousand-class label space via a retriever.
  Also **TechniqueRAG (`arXiv:2505.11988`)** and **H-TechniqueRAG (`arXiv:2604.14166`)**, which
  retrieve candidates and have the LLM re-rank them, and **Semantic Ranking (`arXiv:2403.17068`)**,
  which frames annotation as ranking rather than generation.
  *How I searched:* domain terms first ("ATT&CK technique mapping LLM"), which surfaced only the
  security systems; the decisive hit came from searching the **general-ML formulation**
  ("decouple description from label assignment, retrieval instead of generation, extreme
  multi-label"). Anyone reviewing with security terms alone would miss it.
- **Verdict: `ALREADY DONE BY PRIOR WORK`** — in its general form, and published January 2024.
- The honest position: our contribution is **not the idea**. It is (i) a *stricter* variant —
  in Infer-Retrieve-Rank, TechniqueRAG and H-TechniqueRAG the LM still selects or re-ranks from
  retrieved candidates, whereas ours never lets the model touch an ID at all and gates any
  override; and (ii) an application to a **different input modality** (binary evidence, not
  report prose — G5). Both are defensible as a *domain instantiation with a design refinement*.
  Neither survives being presented as a new idea. The paper must cite Infer-Retrieve-Rank
  prominently and position against it, not around it.

### Claim C5a — ranking and gating are separate axes; compose per-axis winners (hybrid)

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:** `NONE FOUND` for the *metric separation*.
  Semantic Ranking optimises ranking; H-TechniqueRAG's tactic filter is a candidate-space
  reduction (77.5%) rather than a correctness gate; TTPrint's convergent verification is the
  closest architectural analogue but is evaluated end-to-end, not as a separable axis.
  *Searched:* "retrieval ranking vs thresholding distinct metrics", "gate separation", plus each
  system's abstract read for a reported threshold metric.
- **However — a serious partial collision on the *conclusion*:** the Büchel SoK's insight (2),
  *"traditional NLP approaches ... outperform modern embedder-based and generative approaches in
  realistic settings"*, is a stronger and more general statement of what our §1.5.1 found for the
  gating axis (TF-IDF gates cleanly, embeddings do not) and what §1.5.3 found for the case RAG
  (a frequency prior beat the retriever). Published at USENIX Security, August 2025.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY`.** The *metric* — reporting gate separation as an axis independent of
  ranking, and composing per-axis winners — appears unreported and is ours. The *headline*
  ("embeddings are not the answer here") is already the field's published position, and we should
  cite it as corroboration rather than present it as discovery. Missing piece: our N=4,913 result
  needs to be positioned explicitly as a **mechanistic refinement of the SoK's insight (2)**.

### Claim N4 — auto-correcting valid-but-weak IDs is net-negative (38% damaged / 21% recovered)

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:** `NONE FOUND`. The nearest is post-hoc correction in
  the opposite direction (LlmCorr, `arXiv:2402.13414` — LLM corrects an ML model). *Searched:*
  "post-hoc correction harms correct predictions", "label correction regression ablation
  taxonomy", plus each ATT&CK system for a correction stage with a measured cost.
- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED` by prior work — this is ours.** Confidence `MEDIUM` (an absence of
  evidence over abstracts, not proof of absence).
- It is also the most *transferable* of our results: anyone building TechniqueRAG-style
  re-ranking has an implicit override policy and, on this evidence, has not measured its cost.

### Claim N7 — degenerate technique-ID loop; sampler penalties necessary-but-insufficient

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:** `NONE FOUND` for the pathology as such.
  Hallucinated/invalid IDs are addressed (TTPrint's verification; constrained decoding as the
  general remedy), but as an *output-validity* problem, not as a *budget-exhaustion* failure mode.
- **Verdict: `NOT CLOSED` by prior work.** Confidence `MEDIUM`.
- Caveat we must state ourselves: the obvious reviewer response is *"constrained decoding solves
  this"* — and it largely would. Our contribution is the empirical characterisation plus the
  finding that the **cheaper** mitigation (sampler penalties) does not work, which is worth
  reporting precisely because it is what a practitioner reaches for first.

### Gap G1 (local-vs-frontier) and G5 (binary modality)

- **G1 — `NOT CLOSED` by us either.** We run one model on one machine. `arXiv:2606.18166` shows
  parameter size is the only significant predictor of F1 in this task, which makes our
  single-model design a live threat to validity, not a neutral choice. This corroborates our own
  Part E.8 from the outside.
- ~~**G5 — `PARTIALLY CLOSED` and it is our strongest unclaimed asset.**~~ **RETRACTED.**
  **Prior work claiming a similar solution: `arXiv:2602.06325` (TTPDetect).** It maps stripped
  malware binaries to ATT&CK with a deterministic retrieval pre-pass feeding an LLM reasoner —
  architecturally the same shape as ours, published February 2026, at 93.25% function-level
  precision on its own labelled dataset.
  **Verdict: `ALREADY DONE BY PRIOR WORK`.**
  This is the second of our two framing candidates to fall to prior art in one review. What is
  left to us on this axis is genuinely narrow and should be stated as such:
  - TTPDetect appears to demonstrate on a **cloud** model (unverified); our claim is the same
    task on a ~3B-active local model — that difference is real but it is an R7 contribution,
    not an R2 one.
  - TTPDetect reasons at **function level** over decompiled code; ours fuses six deterministic
    Layer-0 detectors, three domain analysts and a corroboration cascade. That is a different
    system, but "different system" is not a contribution until the difference is measured.
  - **G5′ (no shared binary→ATT&CK benchmark) remains open**, and TTPDetect's dataset — if
    released — is the obvious thing to evaluate against. That is now a concrete next step
    rather than a gap we can claim.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

Ranked by research value ÷ effort.

1. ~~**Reframe the paper around input modality (G5).**~~ **WITHDRAWN** — TTPDetect occupies that
   ground. Replaced by: **benchmark against TTPDetect on its own labelled dataset if it is
   public, and report the delta honestly.** *Effort: moderate.* What would close it: a
   like-for-like number on someone else's binary→ATT&CK ground truth — which simultaneously
   answers "your ground truth is your own" (our Part E.4) and establishes whether our
   multi-layer cascade buys anything over TTPDetect's function-level agent. Reviewer's counter:
   *"you lose"* — a real possibility at 93.25% precision, and worth knowing before we build a
   framing on top of it. **Do this early.**

2. **Report gate separation as a first-class metric, and cite the SoK as corroboration (C5a).**
   *Effort: low — the number exists.* What would close it: state the metric formally, show it is
   not derivable from top-k accuracy, and demonstrate on TRAM2 that two systems with equal
   ranking have unequal gates. Reviewer's counter: *"this is just precision at a threshold"* —
   pre-empt with the correct-vs-wrong score-separation framing.

3. **Publish N4 (auto-correction regression) as a standalone caution.** *Effort: low — measured.*
   Highest transfer value of anything we have: every retrieve-then-rerank system has this policy
   and none reports its cost. What would close it: replicate the ablation on TechniqueRAG's own
   setting, not only ours. Reviewer's counter: *"specific to your index"* — hence the replication.

4. **Add a frontier arm (G1).** *Effort: moderate; cost, not research risk.* Run the identical
   pipeline behind one frontier model. What would close it: a delta that separates *architecture*
   from *model capability* — currently inseparable in our results, and `2606.18166` gives the
   prior that size dominates. Reviewer's counter: *"you tuned prompts for the small model"* —
   mitigate by changing nothing but the endpoint.

5. **Evaluate on TTPrint-Bench and/or AnnoCTR.** *Effort: moderate.* Both are public,
   document-level, and post-date our harness. Using an external benchmark someone else built is
   the cheapest available answer to "your ground truth is your own". Reviewer's counter:
   *"those are prose benchmarks; your input is binary"* — which is exactly why this belongs as a
   **secondary** evaluation supporting item 1, not as the main result.

6. **Do not attempt to beat TechniqueRAG/H-TechniqueRAG on CTI-prose F1.** *Effort: high, value:
   negative.* Different task, tuned baselines, and the SoK reports a ceiling nobody is crossing.
   Recording this as an explicit non-goal is worth more than a losing comparison.
