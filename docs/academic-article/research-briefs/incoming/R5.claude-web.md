# R5 — Retrieval-augmented generation for malware analysis and CTI

> **Provenance.** Claude (Opus 5), live web search, 2026-08-08, against the current R5 brief
> including the three standing instructions. Every citation used in the body was resolved by
> fetching its abstract page; unverified leads are quarantined in §5.
>
> **Bottom line up front:** our N6 is **not** the first published negative RAG result in malware
> analysis — `arXiv:2605.03140` (SecDev '26) reports one, by a different mechanism. And the
> "you must compare against a trivial label-only baseline" principle is established general-ML
> methodology with a dedicated 2015 paper showing published work violates it. What survives as
> ours is narrower and, I think, more interesting: **the standard remedy for vocabulary mismatch
> failed**, and the failure was invisible because the similarity score never moved.

---

## 1. State of the art

- **Evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Explainable Malware Analysis** — Jayson Ng,
  Amin Milani Fard. **Poster, ACM SecDev '26**. `arXiv:2605.03140` (4 May 2026)
  - The closest published work to our N6, and it is a **negative result**: *"RAG frequently
    degrades explanation quality by introducing distracting or weakly related context and adding
    narrative noise or generic write-ups."* Its framing is the load-bearing part: *"malware
    explanation is primarily a signal-extraction task, not a knowledge-retrieval problem."*
  - LLM role: explainer; RAG as the treatment under test
  - Model used: **not named in the abstract** (multiple LLMs implied)
  - Evaluated on: corpus not stated in the abstract
  - Headline result: RAG **degrades** explanation quality
  - Rigor flags: baseline? implied non-RAG arm | CIs? unknown | repeats? unknown | stat test?
    unknown — it is a poster, and the abstract carries no numbers
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and the negative finding; **LOW** on everything
    quantitative. Someone must read the poster before we position against it.

- **AsmRAG: LLM-Driven Malware Detection by Retrieving Functionally Similar Assembly Code** —
  `arXiv:2604.23196`
  - Builds a knowledge base of assembly functions embedded by a code-specialised LLM and
    retrieves *functionally similar* assembly, deliberately semantic rather than syntactic so it
    survives obfuscation. Adds a "Density-Weighted Anchor Selection" mechanism to isolate
    malicious logic and extract forensic evidence.
  - LLM role: embedder + detector
  - Model used: **not named**; "code-specialised LLM"
  - Evaluated on: **40,000 binaries**
  - Headline result: **detection F1 96%**, **family-attribution F1 95%**; compared against
    **EMBER and ResNeXt**, with the claim that it degrades less under metamorphic obfuscation
  - Rigor flags: baseline? **yes, two non-LLM baselines** | large N | CIs? not stated |
    stat test? not stated
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and headline numbers; **MEDIUM** on method.
  - **Relevant to our C2**: this is the semantic-retrieval-over-code tier we built as the RAG
    half of the two-tier attribution design, at 40k scale. It maps to family, **not to ATT&CK**.

- **TechniqueRAG** — Lekssays et al., **ACL Findings 2025**, `arXiv:2505.11988`; and
  **H-TechniqueRAG** — Morbiato et al., `arXiv:2604.14166`. Verified in `R2.claude-web.md`.
  Retrieval over ATT&CK technique descriptions with LLM re-ranking; the hierarchical variant
  cuts the candidate space 77.5% via tactic-first filtering (+3.8% F1, −62.4% latency).
  Neither reports a retrieval-free baseline in its abstract.

- **TTPDetect** — `arXiv:2602.06325`. Verified in `R2.claude-web.md`. Dense retrieval + LLM
  neural retrieval to narrow entry points in stripped binaries, then a function-level agent.
  **Built a dataset labelling decompiled functions with TTPs** — see G4 below.

- **Generating Leakage-Free Benchmarks for Robust RAG Evaluation** (*SeedRG*) —
  `arXiv:2605.08838`
  - Names the evaluation defect directly: *"many benchmark datasets, designed to test RAG
    performance, comprise many questions that can already be answered from an LLM's parametric
    memory."* Its remedy is a **knowledge-leakage filter** that excludes instances answerable
    without retrieval, plus synthetic examples unlikely to be in parametric memory.
  - **Note the difference from our argument.** SeedRG cleans the *benchmark* so retrieval is
    genuinely necessary. We argue for keeping the benchmark and adding a *retrieval-free
    control*. Same disease, opposite treatment — and ours is the cheaper one.
  - Confidence: **HIGH** on existence and the quoted framing.

- **Comparing published multi-label classifier performance measures to the ones obtained by a
  simple multi-label baseline classifier** — Metz, Spolaôr, Cherman, Monard. `arXiv:1503.06952`
  (2015)
  - **Not a security paper, and the decisive one for our methodological claim.** Proposes
    `General_B`, a baseline predicting from class information alone, then re-scores the
    published literature against it: *"a considerable number of published results on 10
    frequently used datasets are worse than or equal to the ones obtained by General_B, and for
    one dataset it reaches up to 43% of the dataset published results."*
  - Confidence: **HIGH** — abstract fetched, finding quoted verbatim.

- **The vocabulary-mismatch problem** — foundational IR, not a 2026 discovery. Queries and
  documents that are *lexically different but semantically similar* defeat sparse retrieval;
  the canonical remedies are query/document expansion and, more recently, **dense retrieval,
  which is standardly described as overcoming vocabulary mismatch** by matching in embedding
  space. Sources are numerous and consistent (e.g. document-expansion work `arXiv:2105.00666`,
  the *Pretrained Transformers for Text Ranking* survey `arXiv:2010.06467`). Confidence
  **HIGH** on the concept; **MEDIUM** on any specific citation, none individually fetched.

- **Leads not fetched** — *Beyond RAG for Cyber Threat Intelligence: A Systematic Evaluation of
  Graph-Based and Agentic Retrieval* (`arXiv:2604.11419`), *RAGRank* (`arXiv:2510.20768`,
  PageRank against CTI corpus poisoning), *RAGSec*, *SafeRAG*, PeerJ CS `cs-3371`. See §5.

## 2. Gaps in the literature

- **G1 — Security RAG papers do not report a label-frequency baseline.** `MEDIUM–HIGH`
  - Evidence: 0 of the verified security systems report one; the only paper that does is a
    2015 general-ML methodology audit. The *principle* is textbook, which makes the omission
    more damning, not less.
  - Severity: high where the label distribution is skewed — and ATT&CK label distributions are
    severely skewed (in our own corpus one technique appears in 71% of cases).

- **G2 — Dense retrieval is assumed to solve vocabulary mismatch; nobody reports when it
  doesn't.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: the IR literature positions dense retrieval as *the* remedy. I found no work
    characterising a case where dense embeddings fail because shared boilerplate dominates the
    similarity, nor any reporting the *score distribution* of a deployed retriever to show
    whether it discriminates at all.
  - Severity: moderate and under-appreciated — a retriever that scores everything 0.78–0.90
    looks healthy on every dashboard.

- **G3 — Negative RAG results exist but are single and unquantified in this domain.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: exactly one (`arXiv:2605.03140`), and it is a poster whose abstract carries no
    numbers. Everything else reports gains.
  - Severity: publication-bias territory; a second, mechanistically different negative result
    with numbers is worth something.

- **G4 — A decompiled-code↔ATT&CK corpus now exists but may not be public.** `MEDIUM`
  - Evidence: TTPDetect (`arXiv:2602.06325`) states it built one. Release status unverified.
    Our own dataset survey's conclusion that no such corpus exists is, at best, **dated**.
  - Severity: high for us specifically — it is both a threat to a survey claim and the single
    most useful artifact we could evaluate against.

## 3. Does the described approach close these gaps?

### Claim N6 — a working retriever whose query never reaches it; the frequency prior wins

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution:**
  - On *negative RAG in malware analysis*: **`arXiv:2605.03140`** (SecDev '26) — RAG degrades
    explanation quality; malware explanation is signal extraction, not knowledge retrieval.
  - On *the baseline principle*: **`arXiv:1503.06952`** (2015) — published multi-label results
    routinely fail to beat a label-only baseline, up to 43% on one dataset.
  - On *the failure mode*: the **vocabulary-mismatch problem** is foundational IR.
  *How I searched:* the security framing first, then the general-RAG framing ("when does RAG
  fail", "retrieval-free baseline", "parametric knowledge"), then classical **IR** vocabulary —
  which is where the mismatch concept lives and where a security-only search would never look.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY` — and the surviving part is not the part we have been emphasising.**
  - The *negative-RAG-in-malware* headline: **not first**. Cite `2605.03140`.
  - The *report a frequency prior* methodological point: **not new**, it is textbook and has a
    2015 audit paper. What we can claim is that a 2025–2026 security subfield still omits it,
    with a measured instance where the omission would have hidden a null result.
  - **What is ours:** the specific mechanism. Dense retrieval is the *standard remedy* for
    vocabulary mismatch, and here it did not work — the shared boilerplate between query and
    corpus dominated the embedding, so all 15 production queries scored 0.78–0.90 **regardless
    of content**, and a threshold of 0.35 filtered nothing. The retriever was not weak; it was
    *uninformative and looked healthy*. I found no prior work reporting this shape.
  - **The transferable instrument:** report the retriever's **score distribution** on real
    queries, not just top-k accuracy. A retriever whose scores do not spread is a constant
    wearing a similarity metric — the same conclusion §1.5.1 reached for the semantic ATT&CK
    gate, arrived at independently for a second component.

### Claim C2 — two-tier attribution (exact opcode-hash + semantic RAG)

- **Prior work claiming a similar solution: `arXiv:2604.23196` (AsmRAG).** Semantic retrieval
  over assembly for detection *and family attribution*, at 40,000 binaries, F1 95–96%, against
  EMBER and ResNeXt. That is our Tier-B (semantic) at a scale we cannot match.
- **Verdict: `PARTIALLY` for the semantic tier — `NOT CLOSED` for the composition.** What is
  unaddressed by AsmRAG is the **two-tier** design itself: an exact-opcode-hash tier whose
  precision is near-certain, feeding a *confidence prior* that the semantic tier and the LLM
  then corroborate, with an instruction-count floor to stop tiny thunks manufacturing false
  family links. Nothing I found composes a deterministic exact-match tier with a semantic one
  and reports what each contributes.
- **Caveat we must state:** C2 is still `UNMEASURED` on our side. Against AsmRAG's 40k-binary,
  two-baseline evaluation, an unmeasured design is not a contribution. Either measure it or
  drop it from the claim list.

### Gap G4 — the missing decompiled-code↔ATT&CK corpus

- **Verdict: our survey claim is `SUPERSEDED`.** TTPDetect built such a dataset. Our §4 `SURVEY`
  conclusion — "no source provides a decompiled-code↔ATT&CK corpus" — was true when written and
  must now be dated and qualified rather than repeated.

## 4. How to extend to close the remaining gaps

1. **Publish the score-distribution instrument, not the negative result.** *Effort: low — the
   data exists.* The finding "the frequency prior beat our RAG" is now second in line behind
   `2605.03140`. The finding "**a retriever can be uninformative while every visible metric
   looks fine, and the score distribution is what reveals it**" is not. Report the
   correct-vs-wrong score separation and the fraction of queries clearing the threshold, as a
   diagnostic other builders can run in an afternoon. Reviewer's counter: *"that's just
   calibration"* — pre-empt by showing top-k accuracy stayed healthy while the gate was inert.

2. **Check whether TTPDetect's dataset is released, and evaluate on it.** *Effort: moderate,
   gated on release.* Closes G4, retires a stale survey claim, and gives us an external ground
   truth — which is also our Part E.4 problem. **Highest priority item in this theme.**

3. **Measure C2 or drop it.** *Effort: moderate; needs the LLM for the end-to-end arm, but the
   tier-contribution part is deterministic and could run offline.* Against AsmRAG, an
   unmeasured two-tier claim will not survive review. Minimum bar: what fraction of attributions
   come from the exact tier, from the semantic tier, from both, and what does the
   instruction-count floor actually prevent.

4. **Read the SecDev '26 poster before positioning against it.** *Effort: trivial.* Our
   mechanism (query never reaches the corpus) and theirs (retrieved context dilutes a
   signal-extraction task) are different, and that difference is the whole basis for claiming a
   second contribution rather than a duplicate. It cannot be asserted from an abstract.

5. **Do not build a "RAG for CTI" system contribution.** *Effort: high, value: negative.*
   TechniqueRAG, H-TechniqueRAG, AsmRAG and TTPDetect occupy this ground with large-N
   evaluations. Our RAG material is worth publishing as *evaluation methodology*, not as a
   retrieval system.
