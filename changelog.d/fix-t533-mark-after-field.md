### Fixed

- **A mark the mediator writes right after a line's field is that line's mark (T-533).** A line shaped `[analysts: X] [not blocking: <reason>] Claim N ...` put its mark right after the `[analysts: ...]` field rather than at the end of the line, and `debate_facts.read_marks` read only a mark that ends its line: the line was recorded as a mark not read and blocked, so the analysts it alone named were asked again and a claim it named could be withdrawn by rule though the mediator had said it does not block. A `[blocking ...]` or `[not blocking ...]` mark right after the field, with only other marks or the carried-or-new mark (`[carried: Ln]`, `[new]`) between them, is now read as the line's mark, under the same rules as one that ends the line (a `[not blocking]` with no reason is still no mark); emphasis around the field and around each of those marks is no part of them. A mark that ends the line with a reason is read first and exactly as before; a reasoned mark after the field is the line's over a bare one ending it, unless the line also opens a mark that cannot be read, which still blocks it. The marks after a line's field are read by one pattern fragment, which the reading of the claim numbers after them (T-532) composes too, its reading unchanged. A mark's reason is read possessively, so a reason whose bracket never closes costs one pass over the line, not one per split of it. A line holding marks of both kinds, wherever they stand, is still a mark not read: it blocks, and the run summary and the report say so as for every unread mark.
